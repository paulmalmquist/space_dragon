# Space Dragon — Act III: The Road to Viera (proposal)

**Governing structure (October 30):** [the campaign contract](CAMPAIGN_STRUCTURE.md) gives Viera exactly five missions, ending with the family moving there. The numbered scenes below are proposed beat material, not extra mission slots. Waystation/Drift ideas must fit travel or the existing campaign slots, without adding another campaign planet. Active mission parties have at most three family members, one controlled leader and a mostly autonomous sidekick/button; full-family playable gates must be adapted.

**Date:** October 30, 2026; revised the same day for Morrow
**Status:** PROPOSED narrative treatment for owner review. The staging and mechanics are not confirmed or current implementation scope. Morrow and his approved arc are confirmed; other names retain their individual proposal labels. The final landing on Viera, suit upgrades becoming powers by the end, and dogfights and asteroid fields between planets are CONFIRMED owner direction ([decision](../decisions/2026-10-29-three-acts-linear-and-viera.md)). So is Morrow as the villain who caused the SOS ([decision](../decisions/2026-10-30-villain-is-the-reason-for-the-sos.md), [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md)). How this act delivers them is proposed.
**Builds on:** [Act II](ACT_II_ANSWER_THE_CALL.md), the [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md) and the [Acts II & III overview](ACTS_II_III_THE_ROAD_TO_VIERA.md).
**Scripts:** [Landfall and the Rest Garden](scripts/A3-04_THE_REST_GARDEN.md) and [The Last Hold and the Final Landing](scripts/A3-09_THE_FINAL_LANDING.md). The lane and waypoint scripts (A3-00 to A3-03) are not written yet.

## 1. What Act III must do

- **Land on Viera, for good.** The game's final landing is on Viera, a planet where everyone is a space traveler.
- **Face Morrow.** The villain who caused the SOS is waiting at the heart of Viera. Defeat his note with song, then face him in the last battle, and give him a choice.
- **Bring the neighbor back,** and with her the Bridge, the family's way home.
- **Resolve the Quiet** with understanding, not force. Nothing attacks the Hush.
- **Turn upgrades into powers.** Suits get their biggest upgrade on arrival in Farport, then each family member gains one power by the end. As powers arrive, each family member's instrument voice **morphs into synth** (owner direction, PR #15 / SD-022).
- **Finish composing the answer.** The last worlds add their parts to the SOS arrangement, and the Rest Garden adds the rests and the **Heartnote** (owner direction, PR #15 / SD-020).
- **Keep the flying big.** Dogfights, an asteroid field, an interdiction ambush, a capital-ship trench run, and a final battle against a dragon the size of a mountain.
- **Keep Act I's promise of a way home.** The family can always get back to Earth.
- **Keep the rules:** mythic, not graphic. The family stays together, frightening beats stay short, nobody dies on screen, help never blocks the story, and nothing asks for reading at speed.

> **Logline (proposed):** The last lane runs to Viera, and the family flies it with no way home: the neighbor has taken the Bridge to Morrow. When their own dragon, built in Morrow's likeness, hears him call it *brother* and falls still on the landing field, they cross a city frozen mid-goodbye on foot, to the garden where he lies coiled around his own mother. They break his note with a song he doesn't own. Viera wakes, every ship takes off at once, and Morrow stands up.

## 2. Viera

Viera's lore is drafted in the [overview](ACTS_II_III_THE_ROAD_TO_VIERA.md) § Viera, the lanes and the Quiet, and Morrow's in the [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md). In short:

- Every Vieran is a traveler with a **lane book** of marks from the worlds they've reached. Greeting: "Fair lanes!" Farewell: "Light your way home."
- **Farport**, the capital, is a spaceport city: landing fields, Outfitters' Row, the Departure Halls, the dragon Roosts and, at its heart, the **Rest Garden**.
- **The Mooring Ring** circles the planet: thousands of ships, docked and held. It's Morrow's collection. The Sweeper has been bringing every ship in the universe here.
- **The Lanemother**, the oldest star-dragon, sang the first lane. She lies in the Rest Garden, held still by her own son.
- **Why the Quiet happened:** Morrow, the Lanemother's chosen firstborn, held the Heartnote so nothing could ever leave. Long ago she bound him in a rest, the Hush, and the travelers built the Rest Garden over him and rested there before every journey to keep the seal strong. Over the generations they traveled farther, rested less, and forgot why. The seal cracked, he woke, and this time nobody was ready.

Viera is the largest world in the game. Farport is crossed in a fixed order, but each district is wide enough to explore, wake and stamp.

## 4. Structure

Act III in six movements, in a fixed order.

### Movement 1 — The Long Lane (A3-00 → A3-02)

The dragon flies with its chest dark. The robot iguana sits on Luna's lap, looking out the back. The closer they get, the more often the suits drop a word.

**A3-00 The Snag Net.** Mid-jump, the Sweeper's interdictor net yanks the dragon out of light speed. Dogfight the guard drones and cut the net's four anchors to break free. As the last anchor snaps, gold bands of Morrow's note lash along the lane, just missing. *Feel:* being pulled out of hyperspace into an ambush.

**A3-01 The Waystation.** A ringed moon that used to be the travelers' rest stop: a big mess hall, hangars and a house band held mid-song. The Sweeper has made it its depot, with held ships stacked like a junkyard, waiting to be hauled to Viera.
- On foot, the family's instruments wake the house band, and the level's music comes back with them. The band adds the **bass line** to the answer.
- Free the stacked ships, then beat **the Crane**, the depot's foreman bot (on-foot boss, 2–3 phases, slip–expose–alley-oop).
- In the Crane's office, a crate labeled in gold: the neighbor's old clipboard, from the Forge windowsill, collected and filed. The robot iguana won't leave it behind.
- The dragon learns **Tow-line**. The exit flight tows freed ships out through the hangar.
- Learning stop: **Dock Match** (`shape_matching`, with numbered bays).

**A3-02 The Drift.** A huge asteroid field around Viera's moon, full of ships that tumbled when their pilots were held.
- Thread the field. Dock with held ships, and wake their pilots in short on-foot boarding scenes.
- The star-dragons tow the rescued ships in a growing convoy behind you. Each woken pilot's ship sings a phrase back, and together they become the **countermelody**.
- Learning stop: **Count Them Home** (`counting_objects` and the addition track), at a safe docking pause.

### Movement 2 — Battle of the Mooring Ring (A3-03)

Squadron dogfights above Viera, then a trench run along the Barge to disable its grapple arms. Freed ships drift back to their moorings, and the convoy docks beside them.

**The re-tune.** With the Barge crippled, the Sweeper's core is exposed on its bridge. The robot iguana trots up to it first: a long, gentle beep. The Caller gives it the rule it was missing, the one Morrow's rewrite buried: *"Some things that are still are only resting. And when they're moving, they're going somewhere. You don't collect them. You help them get there."* The gold line through its rule flickers and breaks. The Sweeper powers down, then back up, very politely. It is on the family's side from now on.

The dragon gave everything. Its glow is dim, its wings beat slower, and the held note is so loud here that the family has to shout to hear each other.

*Pacing:* the biggest action yet (intensity 4), followed straight away by the act's quietest stretch.

### Movement 3 — Landfall and Farport (A3-04 → A3-05)

**A3-04 Landfall (the low point).** The star-dragons hang back at the edge of Viera's deep Quiet; they'd be held again. The family's dragon goes on alone. Halfway down, a voice fills the cockpit, warm and close, and it isn't speaking to the family.

> MORROW: "Brother. You came home."

The dragon was built in his likeness, from his own kind's scales, and it hears him. It glides down onto a Farport landing field among the held ships, curls up, and **goes still**. The Core in its chest goes silent with it.
- **It isn't a breakdown.** Its living material is held, like the dragons it was made from. Maria can keep it breathing softly, but she can't wake it alone.
- **The stakes are personal.** Their own dragon, the friend they grew in the cul-de-sac, has been taken by the thing it was modeled on.
- **Gentle, not scary.** The family is together, the toddlers are tired, and it's very far from home. The Caller starts to doze.
- **Maria's beat:** *"Everybody stay together."* Lyla plays a recording **from home**, and the home sound pushes color back in a warm circle. They walk on inside it, and the Caller stays awake inside the circle.
- **Pacing:** about a minute of slow walking before the recording, control never stops, and no one is ever separated.

**A3-05 Farport on foot.** The biggest world in the game, crossed in a fixed order inside the warm circle, which grows as districts wake. Every sign is in plain English with a picture beside it, so nobody has to read to find the way.
- **Outfitters' Row:** the family wakes the outfitters with sound, and each suit gets its big **Farport upgrade**, enough to go deeper into the Quiet. Meanwhile the Dragonwrights armor the still dragon (Scale Armor). Learning stop: **Pattern Fit** (`pattern_repeat`).
- **The Departure Halls:** a giant terminal of travelers held mid-goodbye, arms half-raised to wave, suitcases floating. A whole city stopped at the moment of leaving, because he couldn't bear goodbyes. **Morrow's gaze**, a slow sweep of gold light, moves through the halls; the family slips past it in a hide-and-seek section. Being caught means being held for a moment until someone frees you. No failure. Learning stop: **The Big Board** (`letter_matching`, matching only, plus the reading track).
- **The Roosts:** star-dragon nests, empty and cold, and the path down to the center.
- **The Rest Garden gate:** old words cut in the stone, which Luke reads slowly, with help: *Always rest before you go. Always come home.*

*Design note:* the warm-circle walk is the natural home for the BOOKMARKED Line Leader system: one leader, everyone close. It **must not** require split parties. Without Line Leader it works as a guided walk.

### Movement 4 — The Rest Garden (A3-06 → A3-08)

**The scene.** Morrow lies coiled over the broken seal like a sleeping sun, eyes closed. Curled against him, held still, is the Lanemother: he is holding his own mother. Crushed beneath his coils, hands over its ears, is the Hush. And beside his great head, in her lawn chair, perfectly still and perfectly peaceful, sits the neighbor. The Bridge is in her lap.

The climax keeps its **three phases, Listen, Shelter and Heartnote**, each with a different verb. None of them attacks Morrow, and nothing ever attacks the Hush.

**A3-06 Listen (understanding).**
- **The family does what worked all through Act II: they get loud.** His note swallows it. The Hush curls tighter and color drains. The game lets the player try this. It doesn't work, and nothing is lost. This is the turn.
- **Lyla's Listen:** her visor lets the family **hear what the Hush hears**: Morrow's note, endless and pressing, and under it, very small, the Hush trying to make a rest and failing.
- **The Caller understands** the Old Song at last: *"We stopped resting. That's how he got out. We forgot."*
- **The neighbor turns.** She's held, but she chose to be, so she can still hear. *"It's crying. It's been crying this whole time."* *(beat)* *"This was supposed to feel like peace. This isn't quiet. This is just... holding on."* She stands up out of his hold, the same way she sat down in it: by choosing. She walks to the Hush and sits beside it instead. *"I know that feeling."* Her waltz plays slow and soft, and the Hush's drone harmonizes with it.
- **Gesture choices, with no reading:** *sit down*, *wave hello*, *hum softly*, *give it space*. Every choice is kind, and every choice works.

**A3-07 Shelter (protection).**
- Morrow's eyelid twitches. He throws **shards of his note** at the Hush to press it back down.
- **The family protects the Hush.** Nobody attacks. Maria shields, Luna's goo muffles, Luke tethers, Paul braces a wall, Lyla records each shard into the archive "for later," and alley-oops reach the high ones. The Farport upgrades get their first real use here.
- **Failure moves the story forward.** A shard that reaches the Hush only makes it flinch. The phase ends when the Hush is calm, not when a counter is perfect.
- As the last shard is tucked away, the neighbor holds out the Bridge. *"I believe this is yours."* *(beat)* *"This still doesn't mean I approve of the dragon."*

**A3-08 The Heartnote (song).**
- Each family member plays one soft note; **there are no wrong notes**. Untimed, one tap each, or one tap for everyone.
- The SOS plays one last time, all seven notes with rests, ending on its question. The Caller: *"That's where it always stops. We never knew the end."* Luke: *"Because it's ours."*
- **The family sings their Heartnote**, Earth's, a note Morrow doesn't own, joined by the neighbor and the Caller. After it comes **a rest**, and the Hush rises into it. **A held note can't survive a rest.** Morrow's note breaks.
- Every part the worlds contributed joins in at once: rhythm, harmony, lead, accents, bass and countermelody, now with **rests**. The answer is complete.
- **Payoff:** the universe resumes mid-sentence. Up on the landing field, the family's dragon wakes first, loudest, then rests, and glides down to them. **The Lanemother wakes.** Color floods out from Viera along every lane. The Hush settles into the pauses, given back its place.
- **And Morrow opens his eyes.** *"...No."*

### Movement 5 — Your Own Note and the Last Hold (A3-09 → A3-10)

**A3-09 Your Own Note.** As the song comes back, each suit's living material hears its wearer's own note and blooms. The upgrades fuse into **one power each**, and each family member's instrument voice morphs into its **synth** form (PR #15 / SD-022): a new song, sounds the old song never had, which Morrow can't hold. The motif stays recognizable; only the timbre changes (drafts in the [overview](ACTS_II_III_THE_ROAD_TO_VIERA.md) § Suits to powers). The Lanemother gives the dragon **Lane Wings**. Five Together becomes **Five Powers**, and its chord resolves to the Heartnote for the first time.

**A3-10 The Great Launch and the Last Hold.** Every traveler on Viera wakes and does what Vierans do: they go. The sky fills with ships, all leaving at once, Morrow's worst fear made real. Morrow stands up for the first time in the game. *"STAY."*
- **Phase 1, the sky:** he hurls gold Holds across the sky, catching ships mid-launch. The family's dragon leads the four star-dragons, the hatchlings, the re-tuned Sweeper and every traveler ship. Song-breath breaks each Hold, and every freed ship is one more that gets to leave.
- **Phase 2, his back:** the dragon lands on him. On foot, with their new powers, the family climbs toward his throat, where the stolen Heartnote burns. Paul's Anchor holds a wing still; Luke's Lane Weaver rails them across; Luna's Splashdown surfs them up his spine; Maria's Sanctuary shelters them from his gaze; Lyla's Echo copies whatever the moment needs.
- **Phase 3, the choice:** at his throat, nobody strikes. Lyla plays the Old Song. Luke offers him a way out: *"You could come with us. You could go somewhere too."* The Lanemother calls him by his name. He lets go of the Heartnote, and the Great Song resolves across the whole universe. But he has held for so long he can no longer move. He rises into the sky and becomes a single star that never moves: **the Lodestar**, the star every traveler will steer by to find the way home.

Every attack in this battle targets his hold: the bands, the shards, the stolen note. Never his body. Full draft dialogue is in the [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md) §7 and the [script](scripts/A3-09_THE_FINAL_LANDING.md).

This is the last action beat of the game and its biggest, with every friend the family made flying beside them.

### Movement 6 — The Final Landing (A3-11)

- **Formation home.** The dragon leads the whole fleet down to Farport under the new star. It's the victory lap: no enemies, formation calls, every ring adding an instrument.
- **Lane books.** On the landing field, every family member's book gets Viera's mark. The Caller's family finds them, and the Caller gets their very first mark, because they came home from their first voyage. On the last page of every lane book, a small star has appeared.
- **The dragon chooses.** It's home among its makers and its kind, under the star of the dragon it was modeled on. It could stay. It looks at the star-dragons, at the Lanemother, at the Lodestar, then at the family, and lowers its wing.
- **The neighbor's chair** stays in the Rest Garden. Vierans start sitting in it.
- **The way home.** The Bridge goes back into the dragon's chest, and the Lanemother sings a permanent lane to Earth as thanks. The family can go home whenever they like, and come back.
- **The Lanemother, to Paul and Maria:** *"Hold them. And then let them go. Both. That is the whole song."*
- **Closing image:** Farport at night, travelers resting in the Rest Garden for the first time in ages, the family on a blanket, the dragon curled around them. Overhead, the Lodestar, still and bright, and beside it the lane home to Earth glowing steady. When the dragon hums, faraway voices hum back.

**Post-credits:** the cul-de-sac block party, some time later. A small Vieran ship comes down the lane and lands on the street. It's the Caller, on their second voyage, carrying a cake-sized gift for the baker neighbor.

**Post-game (proposed):** the lanes stay open. Free travel between every world, Earth included, with lane-book stamps left to find. This reconciles with v0.1's canon C02, "explores the universe," only after the SOS is answered, which keeps Act I's rule against a sightseeing campaign before it.

## 5. Theme check

| Act | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Will we help? | **Yes.** We weren't chosen, but we choose to. |
| II | *How* do we help? | By making noise where it's needed, waking one world at a time, and refusing to stay when staying is offered. |

The one who was chosen held on and lost everything; the ones who weren't chosen let go and brought everyone home. ## 6. Gameplay vocabulary for Act III (proposed)

| Element | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Lane levels** | Snag Net (interdiction ambush), Waystation exit (tow through a hangar), the Drift (asteroid field, docking), Mooring Ring (dogfight plus capital-ship trench run), the Last Hold (final battle), Formation home (victory lap). One pilot, no crew roles. |
| **Tow-line** | Dragon upgrade from the Waystation: tow ships, or yank a gunship's shield off. |
| **Docking and boarding** | Short on-foot scenes inside held ships in the Drift. |
| **The warm circle** | The home recording's circle of color at Landfall. It moves with the family and grows as Farport's districts wake. |
| **Morrow's gaze** | A hide-and-seek section in the Departure Halls. Caught means held for a moment, never failure. |
| **Holds and hold-shards** | Morrow's gold bands and the fragments of his note. Song-breath breaks Holds in the air; Sanctuary frees held allies; shards are tucked away in Shelter. |
| **Farport upgrades** | One big upgrade per suit from Outfitters' Row, plus Scale Armor for the dragon. One extra slot per suit stays open on purpose. |
| **Powers** | One per family member at A3-09, first used in the Last Hold. Each sounds in its owner's synth voice (PR #15 / SD-022). They always switch off, helmets always open, and the suit never controls its wearer. |
| **Five Powers** | Five Together's final form. Its chord resolves to the Heartnote. |
| **The Last Hold** | Final boss, Morrow, in three phases: the sky, his back, the choice. Phase progress is saved; the choice waits for the player. |
| **The Hush** | **Not a fight.** Listen → Shelter → Heartnote. Nothing attacks it. |

## 7. Learning in Act III (proposed, follows [learning rules](../learning/LEARNING_RULES.md))

Two tracks per stop, as in the [curriculum puzzle plan](../learning/CURRICULUM_PUZZLE_PLAN.md). Signs, boards and bays use plain English letters, numbers and words.

| Where | Activity | Skill (proposed ID) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waystation | **Dock Match:** match each ship's badge to its docking bay | `shape_matching`, numerals or words |
| The Drift | **Count Them Home:** count the rescued ships joining the convoy | `counting_objects`, addition |
| Outfitters' Row | **Pattern Fit:** repeat a color-and-shape pattern to tune an upgrade | `pattern_repeat` |
| Departure Halls | **The Big Board:** find the gate by matching its letter, or reading its word | `letter_matching` (matching only), reading |
| Rest Garden | **The Heartnote:** creative, no right answer | — |

Each is paused and untimed, has an assisted path, and gives the same story reward. None happen during flight or in the Last Hold. No power replaces or gates a learning challenge.

## 9. Production boundaries

- **Nothing here is current scope.** Act III depends on flight, large-world walking, boarding scenes and possibly Line Leader, none of which exist.
- Viera can shrink for production: Farport's districts can collapse to two (Outfitters' Row and the Rest Garden) without changing the story.
- No multiplayer, backend or new external service is implied. Every new character is a scripted scene character.
- All names are placeholders. The neighbor must stay fictional per existing rules.

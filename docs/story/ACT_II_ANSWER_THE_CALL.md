# Space Dragon — Act II: Answer the Call (proposal)

**Date:** October 28, 2026  
**Status:** PROPOSED narrative treatment for owner review. Not CONFIRMED canon, not current implementation scope. All names are placeholders.  
**Builds on:** [Act I](ACT_I_BUILD_THE_DRAGON.md) (authoritative) and the [Act I refinements](ACT_I_CRITIQUE_AND_REFINEMENTS.md) (proposed). Where Act II relies on a refinement, such as the SOS melody, the passenger creature, the Bridge or the neighbor's grievance, it says so. If a refinement is rejected, the dependent beat needs revisiting.

## 1. What Act II must do

From Act I: *"Travel to the source of the SOS and discover who called, what happened, and what help is needed. Do not add an unrelated sightseeing campaign between assembly and answering the signal."*

Additional constraints carried forward:

- The destination is **another universe**, reached by crossing, not by light speed alone.
- The family was **not chosen**. Nothing in Act II may retroactively reveal a bloodline, prophecy or secret design.
- Danger is exciting but recoverable. Nobody dies, and sleeping is not dying.
- The neighbor did **not** cause the emergency.
- The family's significance comes from choices, which Act II should keep testing.

> **Logline (proposed):** On the far side of the crossing, a whole universe has gone quiet: colors faded, songs stopped, its people and its star-dragons asleep. Only visitors from somewhere else can still make a sound. A loud, messy family from a Florida cul-de-sac turns out to be exactly what's needed. Their noise brings things back, and their kindness reaches the thing that made the quiet.

---

## 2. The central idea: the Quiet

**What happened (proposed):** the senders' universe is held together by **song**. Living star-dragons carry music between worlds, and that music keeps light, color and motion going. Something pulled a silence over it all, called **the Quiet**. Anything inside it slowly stills: color drains, sound goes flat, and people and creatures fall into a deep, gently glowing sleep.

This idea answers several OPEN questions from Act I at once.

| Act I open question | Proposed answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why send an SOS *outside* their universe, to anyone in range? | Anyone from inside falls asleep in the Quiet. They needed someone from *elsewhere*, whose own universe's "sound" they carry with them. Earth was in range. That's all. |
| Why is the SOS a melody? (refinement §3.1) | Song is how their world works. A song is the only kind of call they know how to make. |
| Why is the rescue vehicle a *dragon*? | It's grown in the shape of the star-dragons, from their own **living material** (see below). The senders made a new one because the living ones were asleep. **The design existed long before the family**, which fits Act I canon exactly. |
| Why was the equipment so weak when it landed? | Its living material had already started falling asleep in the Quiet before it was sent. Crossing out of the Quiet and being around a noisy family is what woke it up. |
| Why five seats/suits? | Star-dragon crews traditionally fly as a *pod* of five, one for each part of the song. This is proposed lore. It describes the senders' culture and makes no claim about the family. |
| What help do they expect from unknown responders? | Not strength or genius. **Just someone who can still make a sound**, and who will choose to use it. |

### The living material comes from here

This builds on the owner's idea that the material is alive ([Act I refinements §3.4](ACT_I_CRITIQUE_AND_REFINEMENTS.md)); the details are PROPOSED. The living material is **shed star-dragon scale**. Star-dragons naturally shed it, the way a snake sheds skin, and it keeps a little of the dragons' life and song. The senders grew the Core, the suits and the mecha dragon from it.

- **It's never harvested from a hurt or sleeping dragon.** Shedding is natural and painless. This guardrail is required for a family audience.
- **Your dragon is family to the real ones.** When it meets a sleeping star-dragon, its material recognizes home. It hums, glows and pulls toward it. The waking scenes become reunions as much as rescues.
- **The suits feel the Quiet first.** Suit glows dim and the hum goes flat on arrival. The family can *see* that their equipment is in danger here, and keeping it awake is part of the job.

**Why it fits the family:** the thing this family does best is *noise*. Kids shouting, laughing, singing, stomping and arguing about whose turn it is all become the superpower. A five-year-old should understand it instantly: *"So being loud is GOOD here?"*

**Core visual/gameplay mechanic, "Sound paints color":** the world starts grey-blue and muted. The family's footsteps, voices, suit effects and the dragon's roar push color back out around them in ripples. Restored areas stay restored, which is visible, persistent progress that needs no reading.

## 4. The neighbor in Act II (proposed)

The v0.1 spec imagined the neighbor's schemes "following the family into space." Act I forbids making her the cause of the emergency. This proposal satisfies both.

- **She stows away.** After her "somebody has to keep an eye on things" line (refinement §6), she sneaks aboard. Officially she means to "file a formal complaint with whoever is in charge of this dragon." She's discovered mid-crossing, hiding in a storage bay with a folding chair and a clipboard. *(One alternative: one of her robot iguanas stows away instead, and she follows later.)*
- **She's a comic fish out of water.** She complains about the noise *in a universe that's dying of silence*. She tries to file her complaint with sleeping aliens. Her reprogrammed robot iguana becomes a reluctant helper, the first ex-henchman to change sides.
- **She has an arc.** She's the only person in the story who truly understands wanting things to just **be quiet**, and that becomes the key to the climax (§6).
- **Gameplay:** she isn't playable and has no follower AI. She shows up in scenes, stays in the dragon, and comments over the radio. That keeps Act II free of a sixth-character system.

If the owner prefers the neighbor to stay on Earth, the stowaway can be the robot iguana alone, and the climax beat in §6 moves to the Caller.

## 5. Structure

Act II in five movements. As in Act I, destination order is flexible in the middle.

### Movement 1 — The Crossing (A2-00 → A2-01)

**A2-00 Through the ring.** The first real flight: the dragon leaves the cul-de-sac, climbs, makes a light-speed run to the opening, then **crosses** using the Bridge. Light speed and crossing look and feel different. Light speed is streaks and acceleration. Crossing is a shimmer where sound briefly reverses and colors invert. The stowaway is discovered here.

*Production note:* this is where v0.1's "Launch Day" flight prototype finds its narrative home: guided departure, assisted steering, a skippable jump. It can be built and tested separately long before the rest of Act II.

**A2-01 Arrival in the Quiet.** They emerge somewhere muted. The radio crackles silently, the dragon's roar comes out muffled, and the colors are washed out. Luke yells "HELLO?" and color ripples out from the dragon. The first lesson is that **sound brings things back**.

### Movement 2 — The Last Beacon (A2-02)

The passenger leads them to a lone lighthouse-like tower still pulsing the SOS. Inside is **the Caller**, hoarse from singing.

- Through the Core, the Caller explains the Quiet, the sleeping star-dragons and the mechanical dragon they built in their image. They sent it out *"to anyone who could still hear."*
- The family realizes their dragon is a copy of real ones, and that the real ones are asleep.
- The Caller asks for help: **wake the star-dragons, so the song can start again.**
- The neighbor tries to file her complaint with the Caller. The Caller has no idea what a complaint is.

### Movement 3 — Waking the Worlds (A2-03 → A2-06)

Several drifting worlds each hold a **sleeping star-dragon** and a stilled community. Each mirrors an Act I place, so the structure echoes ("these are their schools and stores and workshops") and young players recognize patterns.

| Destination (placeholder) | Mirrors | Featured | What's wrong | How the family helps | What it restores |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Market Moon** | The stores | Paul & Luna | Merchants asleep mid-trade, stalls tipped over, the moon spinning slowly off course. | Paul rights the giant stalls. Luna goo-slides crates into place. A cart race through the restored bazaar. | The **Market Dragon** wakes, and the bazaar reawakens in a wave of noise. |
| **The Hatchery Nebula** | The preschool | Maria & Lyla | Star-dragon eggs going cold. The keepers have fallen asleep on their nests. | Maria warms and heals each egg. Lyla records each egg's faint heartbeat-song and plays it back so they hatch. | **Hatchlings** follow the dragon in a little flock, and the passenger finds its family here (the emotional midpoint). |
| **The Climbing Spires** | Luke's school | Luke | Tall crystal towers that hold the song's echo. Their resonators are all out of tune, and the top is far above the Quiet. | Luke climbs, tethers and re-strikes each resonator. A rhythm and pattern activity, paused and safe, never mid-climb. | The **Spire Dragon** wakes and the echoes return. Now the song can be *heard* across worlds. |
| **The Forge Ring** | The rocket facility | Paul, then everyone | The shipyard where the mechanical dragon was designed. Its great beacon is jammed, and Hush-moths swarm it (§7). | Five-suit teamwork set piece. Lyla finds the original blueprints: **proof that the design is older than the family**, with five seats drawn in. | The **Forge Dragon** wakes. The mechanical dragon gets an upgrade (e.g. a song-breath ability). |

As in Act I, story beats are delivered by **number of dragons woken**, so order doesn't matter. Each world needs only one compact explorable area. This is a production shrink lever: two worlds can carry the act if needed.

**Running gag:** the neighbor's complaints get less sincere. On the Market Moon she haggles. In the Hatchery she's caught knitting a tiny scarf for a cold egg and denies it furiously.

### Movement 4 — The Silent Center (A2-07, the low point)

With four dragons awake, the song is strong enough to reach the center of the universe, where the Elder Dragon sleeps and the Quiet is thickest.

- The dragon flies in. The color drains away, and then **the dragon itself goes silent**. It isn't a breakdown. Its living material is **falling asleep**, like the dragons it was made from. It lands gently on a dark plain, curls up and closes its eyes. The suits' sounds fade too. The family is on foot, and every step is harder.
- **The stakes are personal now.** The family isn't only saving strangers. Their own dragon, the friend they grew in the cul-de-sac, needs waking. Maria can keep it breathing softly with her healing, but she can't wake it alone.
- This is the low point, and it should be gentle rather than scary. Nobody is in danger of falling asleep yet, because they're still from somewhere else. But it's dark and quiet and far from home, and the toddlers are tired.
- **Maria's beat:** she gathers everyone close, like Act I's "Everybody stay together." Lyla plays something from the Family Archive: not the SOS, but a recording **from home**, perhaps the install-party noise, the neighbors cheering, or the family's own bedtime song. The *home* sound is what pushes color back. They walk forward together inside its circle.

*Design note:* this is the natural place for the BOOKMARKED Line Leader system if it exists by then: walking as a party, one leader, everyone close. It **must not** require split parties. If Line Leader isn't built, this works as a guided walk.

### Movement 5 — The Hush and the Answer (A2-08 → A2-10)

**A2-08 The Hush.** At the center, curled around the sleeping Elder Dragon, is **the Hush**. It's enormous, soft and cloud-like, with its "hands" over its "ears." It isn't attacking. It's *hiding*.

**What it is (proposed):** a creature, or a part of the universe itself, that the song overwhelmed. The music got too loud and too much, all the time, everywhere, and like a small child with too much noise, it pulled a blanket of silence over everything. It didn't mean to put everyone to sleep. It just wanted it to stop.

**A2-09 Not a fight.** The family's instinct, and the player's, is to get loud. It works on Hush-moths, but here it makes the Hush curl tighter and the Quiet spread. **Loud is the wrong answer here.** This reverses the act's own rule, and it's the turn.

- **The neighbor steps forward.** She's the one person here who knows exactly how the Hush feels. She sits down next to it and, in her own grumpy way, says she gets it. Sometimes everything is too loud and nobody asks if you're okay. *(This echoes Act I's refinement: nobody asked her whether she was okay when the piece fell in her yard.)*
- The family realizes the song doesn't need to be **loud**. It needs to be **gentle**.
- **Interaction:** a calm, untimed music moment. Each family member plays one soft note, chosen from a few, with no wrong answer: a creative, not graded, activity per the learning rules. The Hush uncovers its ears.

**A2-10 The answering note.** The SOS melody from Act I has always ended on a question. Now the family, all five and maybe the neighbor too, **sing the answer**: a final note the senders never wrote, because it had to come from whoever answered.

- *This is the payoff of the whole two-act arc.* The SOS was a call. The family's note is the answer. **They weren't chosen. They chose to help, and the help was something only they could add.**
- The Elder Dragon wakes, and so does *their* dragon. It wakes first, and it is the loudest. Color floods outward from the center through every world. The woken dragons answer in chorus, and the Hush, comforted, becomes a soft, quiet place the song now flows *around* rather than through. Every song needs rests, and this is one. The Hush is not defeated or destroyed.

### Coda — Home (A2-11)

- The Caller and the senders thank the family. The passenger stays with its family and gives the kids a keepsake, perhaps a small glowing scale that hums the answering note.
- **The dragon chooses too.** It's alive and among its own kind now, so it could stay. The Caller says it's the dragon's choice, and nobody makes it for the dragon. It looks at the star-dragons, then at the family, and lowers its wing for them to climb aboard. This echoes the family's own choice in Act I.
- The Bridge stays open, and the way home and back is promised. **The return trip is shown on screen**, which confirms the Act I promise.
- The cul-de-sac: the dragon lands to a block party. The baking neighbor has made the biggest cake yet. The neighbor walks back across the street, stops and says: *"…It's actually very peaceful up there."* Then she adds: *"Still not approving the dragon."*

**Act II closing image:** the family on the front lawn at night, the dragon curled up in the cul-de-sac, and in the sky the opening, now a steady ring rather than a fading star. When the dragon hums, faraway voices hum back.

## 6. Theme check

| Act | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Will we help? | **Yes.** We weren't chosen, but we choose to. |
| II | *How* do we help? | Not by being the strongest or the loudest. By listening, by bringing home with us, and by being kind even to the thing that caused the problem. |

The neighbor's two-act arc rhymes with the Hush. In Act I the family learns that an "antagonist" might have a real grievance. In Act II that lesson is exactly what saves the universe. And the neighbor is the one who delivers it.

## 7. Gameplay vocabulary for Act II (proposed)

| Element | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Flight between worlds** | Finally the dragon's real playground: **fighter-jet flight** with Cadet, Pilot and Ace assist tiers, dogfights, strike runs, formation flight with woken star-dragons, and a skippable light-speed transition. See the [combat system](../gameplay/COMBAT_SYSTEM.md) §6. |
| **Sound paints color** | A persistent world-restoration layer. Suit abilities, voices and the dragon's roar push color outward, and restored areas stay restored across saves. |
| **Hush-moths** (enemy) | Small grey fluttering things that soak up sound and dim color. Noise, goo or a song-breath scatters them, and they flutter off harmlessly. Simple, readable, never scary. |
| **Still-shells** (obstacle) | Things frozen in place by the Quiet. Each suit "wakes" them a different way: lift, heal, goo, climb to the resonator, or scan for the right note. |
| **Ex-henchman iguana** | The neighbor's robot iguana, now a helper that fetches small items. It changes sides because the living material inside it (from the Bridge piece) recognizes home. It's a scripted helper, not a follower system. |
| **The answering note** | The finale's creative music moment. It's untimed, has no wrong answer, and works for every player age. |

Racing (the Market Moon cart race, a dragon race through restored spires with the woken dragons) and playful combat (Hush-moth swarms, a Forge Ring set piece) stay fun in their own right, with learning moments kept separate and paused.

## 8. Learning in Act II (proposed, follows [learning rules](../learning/LEARNING_RULES.md))

- **Glyph matching:** the senders write in glyphs, and the Core shows each glyph next to a familiar letter. Matching them is `letter_matching` practice. It is never recorded as letter-name recognition, and it always has an assisted path.
- **Rhythm and pattern:** re-striking resonators and hatching eggs with heartbeat-songs both involve repeating a pattern. The challenges are paused and untimed, and the hints repeat audio and narrow the choices.
- **Counting:** count hatchlings, crates and moths. Use small, tangible quantities.
- **Colors and names:** as color returns, the Core asks the child to name it. This is optional, and color is never the only answer cue.
- **Creative, no right answer:** the answering note, and lullaby notes for the Hush.
- As in Act I, required story beats never depend on independent correctness, and help never withholds a dragon, a world or the ending.

## 9. Open decisions

- Accept or reject the core premise (the Quiet, song as the senders' world-force, living star-dragons as the mecha's model).
- Whether the living material is shed star-dragon scale, and whether the dragon's choice to stay with the family is part of the ending.
- Whether the neighbor stows away, sends only a robot iguana, or stays home.
- The Hush's nature: a creature, a phenomenon or a piece of the universe. Its look.
- The Caller's design, name and species. Whether there are adult senders on-screen at all.
- The number of worlds (four proposed, two as a minimum) and their final names.
- Whether Act II ends with a permanent two-way bridge, which would enable a post-game "explore the universe" mode and reconcile with v0.1 canon C02, or whether the bridge closes.
- What Act III, if any, would be: other universes, other calls, or the senders visiting the cul-de-sac.

## 10. Production boundaries

- **Nothing here is current scope.** [The current milestone](../plans/CURRENT_MILESTONE.md) is SD-M0, with SD-M1 First Contact proposed next. Act II depends on flight, walking exploration and possibly Line Leader, none of which exist.
- The cheapest high-value slice to prototype early is **A2-00 The Crossing**, as the separately labeled flight prototype that Act I already permits.
- No multiplayer, backend or new external service is implied. The neighbor, the Caller, the passenger and the iguana are scripted scene characters, not followers or network players.
- All destination and character names are placeholders. The neighbor must stay fictional per existing rules.

# Space Dragon — Act II: Answer the Call (proposal)

**Date:** October 28, 2026; rewritten October 30, 2026 for the three-act structure  
**Status:** PROPOSED narrative treatment for owner review. Not CONFIRMED canon, not current implementation scope. All names are placeholders. The linear three-act structure and Viera as the final landing are CONFIRMED owner direction ([decision](../decisions/2026-10-29-three-acts-linear-and-viera.md)), and so is Morrow as the villain who caused the SOS ([decision](../decisions/2026-10-30-villain-is-the-reason-for-the-sos.md), [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md)); how this act serves them is proposed.  
**Builds on:** [Act I](ACT_I_BUILD_THE_DRAGON.md) (authoritative), the [Act I refinements](ACT_I_CRITIQUE_AND_REFINEMENTS.md) (proposed) and the [Acts II & III overview](ACTS_II_III_THE_ROAD_TO_VIERA.md) (proposed). Where Act II relies on a refinement, such as the SOS melody, the passenger creature, the Bridge or the neighbor's grievance, it says so. If a refinement is rejected, the dependent beat needs revisiting.  
**Continues in:** [Act III: The Road to Viera](ACT_III_THE_ROAD_TO_VIERA.md), which now holds the low point, the Hush climax and the ending that earlier versions of this document carried.

## 1. What Act II must do

From Act I: *"Travel to the source of the SOS and discover who called, what happened, and what help is needed. Do not add an unrelated sightseeing campaign between assembly and answering the signal."*

Constraints carried forward:

- The destination is **another universe**, reached by crossing, not by light speed alone.
- The family was **not chosen**. Nothing in Act II may retroactively reveal a bloodline, prophecy or secret design.
- Danger is exciting but recoverable. Nobody dies, and sleeping is not dying.
- The neighbor did **not** cause the emergency.
- The family's significance comes from choices, which Act II keeps testing.

From the [three-act decision](../decisions/2026-10-29-three-acts-linear-and-viera.md):

- The route is **linear**: one fixed order of worlds.
- The dragon carries the family **planet to planet**, with **dogfights and asteroid fields** on the way.
- Act II is the middle act. It must end with a turn that sends the family on toward **Viera**, the final landing, in Act III.
- Suits begin to grow here with small **upgrades**; powers wait for the end of Act III.

From the [villain decision](../decisions/2026-10-30-villain-is-the-reason-for-the-sos.md) and the owner's approval of [Morrow](VILLAIN_ARC.md):

- **Morrow** is the reason for the SOS. In Act II he goes from a story to a voice to his first victory, at the Forge.
- The tone is mythic, not kiddy, and still not graphic.

From later owner direction (October 29–30, recorded as SD-020 and SD-022):

- The planet-to-planet missions **compose the SOS**: each world adds a part to the answering arrangement.
- As upgrades become powers, the family's instrument voices **morph into synth**. That happens in Act III.

> **Logline (proposed):** On the far side of the crossing, a whole universe stands frozen mid-moment, held inside one endless note by a fallen dragon who couldn't bear for anything to leave. Only visitors from somewhere else can still move and make a sound. A loud, messy family from a Florida cul-de-sac wakes four star-dragons, one world at a time, each one lighting the path to the next. At the end of the path is Viera, where the fallen dragon waits. And when he finally speaks to them, he makes them an offer.

## 2. The central idea: the Quiet

**What happened (proposed):** the senders' universe is held together by **song**. Living star-dragons sing **song-lanes** between worlds, and that music keeps light, color and motion going. Every song in it comes home to one note, the **Heartnote**. Morrow, the Lanemother's firstborn, was chosen to carry it. When the travelers kept leaving, he closed his jaws on the Heartnote and **held** it, and everything that sings the song stopped where it stood. That held note is **the Quiet**. It isn't silence: it's one note so endless it swallows every other sound. Anything alive inside it is held mid-moment, glowing faintly, unharmed. Machines keep running. See the [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md).

Act II shows the Quiet and fights it world by world. The family learns who made it at Lantern Rock and hears him for the first time at the Spires. They face his offer at the Forge, and face him at last in [Act III](ACT_III_THE_ROAD_TO_VIERA.md).

This idea answers several OPEN questions from Act I at once.

| Act I open question | Proposed answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why send an SOS *outside* their universe, to anyone in range? | Everything in this universe shares the Heartnote, and Morrow holds it, so he can hold everything that sings it. Only someone whose Heartnote lives somewhere else can move inside his note. Earth was in range. That's all. |
| Why does the SOS never finish? | Its last note is the Heartnote, and Morrow holds it. Nobody in this universe can sing it, so the answer has to come from someone with a home of their own. |
| Why is the SOS a melody? (refinement §3.1) | Song is how their world works. A song is the only kind of call they know how to make. |
| Why is the rescue vehicle a *dragon*? | It's grown in the shape of the star-dragons, from their own **living material** (see below). The shipwrights of the Forge Ring finished an old design because the living dragons were asleep. **The design existed long before the family**, which fits Act I canon exactly. |
| Why was the equipment so weak when it landed, and why did it scatter? | Its living material had already started to go still in the Quiet before it was sent. As it crossed, Morrow's note reached through the closing opening and struck it, and the bundle broke apart over Earth. Being around a noisy family is what woke it up. |
| Why five seats/suits? | The design is Morrow's likeness as he was before he fell: the first dragon to carry travelers, five of them, on the first voyage. Star-dragon crews have flown as a *pod* of five ever since. This describes the senders' history and makes no claim about the family. |
| What help do they expect from unknown responders? | Not strength or genius. **Just someone who can still make a sound**, and who will choose to use it. |
| Who sent it, and why? | The people of **Viera**, a planet where everyone is a space traveler, because Morrow had stopped their universe. Its youngest, the Caller, kept the SOS going from Lantern Rock, the farthest lighthouse on the lanes and the last place his note reached. |
| Why is the journey one fixed route? | A song-lane only lights up when the star-dragon at its far end is awake. Each dragon the family wakes opens the way to the next world, and every lane ends at Viera. |

### The living material comes from here

This builds on the owner's idea that the material is alive ([Act I refinements §3.4](ACT_I_CRITIQUE_AND_REFINEMENTS.md)); the details are PROPOSED. The living material is **shed star-dragon scale**. Star-dragons naturally shed it, the way a snake sheds skin, and it keeps a little of the dragons' life and song. The shipwrights grew the Core, the suits and the mecha dragon from it.

- **It's never harvested from a hurt or sleeping dragon.** Shedding is natural and painless. This guardrail is required for a family audience.
- **Your dragon is family to the real ones.** When it meets a sleeping star-dragon, its material recognizes home. It hums, glows and pulls toward it. The waking scenes become reunions as much as rescues.
- **The suits feel the Quiet first.** Suit glows dim and the hum goes flat on arrival, because the suits are made of star-dragon scale, which belongs to Morrow's song. The family doesn't. They can *see* that their equipment is in danger here, and keeping it awake is part of the job.
- **Scale gifts.** Each star-dragon the family wakes sheds a fresh scale for the family members featured on its world. The suit absorbs it and gains one small upgrade. This is the first step of the suits-to-powers arc that ends in Act III.

**Why it fits the family:** the thing this family does best is *noise*. Kids shouting, laughing, singing, stomping and arguing about whose turn it is all become the superpower. A five-year-old should understand it instantly: *"So being loud is GOOD here?"* Act III will complicate that lesson; Act II gets to enjoy it.

**Core visual/gameplay mechanic, "Sound paints color":** the world starts grey-blue and muted. The family's footsteps, voices, suit effects and the dragon's roar push color back out around them in ripples. Restored areas stay restored, which is visible, persistent progress that needs no reading. On large worlds, each restored zone also **wakes its locals**, who become guides and side-quest givers, so every world gets louder and fuller as it's played.

## 4. The neighbor in Act II (proposed)

The v0.1 spec imagined the neighbor's schemes "following the family into space." Act I forbids making her the cause of the emergency. This proposal satisfies both.

- **She stows away.** After her "somebody has to keep an eye on things" line (refinement §6), she sneaks aboard. Officially she means to "file a formal complaint with whoever is in charge of this dragon." She's discovered mid-crossing, hiding in the storage bay with a folding chair and a clipboard.
- **She's a comic fish out of water.** She complains about the noise *in a universe that's dying of silence*. She tries to file her complaint with sleeping aliens. Her reprogrammed robot iguana becomes a reluctant helper, the first ex-henchman to change sides.
- **She gets a seatmate.** From Lantern Rock on, the Caller rides in the storage bay with her: the kid who has never traveled and the grump who never wanted to. Their radio commentary runs through every flight.
- **She has an arc.** She's the only person in the story who truly understands wanting things to just **be quiet**. Through Act II she softens in small moments. Then at the Forge, Morrow offers her exactly what she has always wanted: quiet, forever, with nobody ever touching anything again. **She says yes**, takes the Bridge from the dragon's chest, and goes to him. In Act III she's the one who turns back, and that turn saves everyone.
- **Gameplay:** she isn't playable and has no follower AI. She shows up in scenes, stays in the dragon, and comments over the radio. That keeps Acts II and III free of a sixth-character system.

## 5. Structure

Act II in four movements, in a fixed order. Each world is a large, landable place, and every lane between worlds is a flight level.

### Movement 1 — The Crossing (A2-00)

**A2-00 Through the ring.** The first real flight: the dragon leaves the cul-de-sac, climbs, makes a light-speed run to the opening, then **crosses** using the Bridge. Light speed and crossing look and feel different. Light speed is streaks and acceleration. Crossing is a shimmer where sound briefly reverses and colors invert. The stowaway is discovered here.

*Production note:* this is where v0.1's "Launch Day" flight prototype finds its narrative home: guided departure, assisted steering, a skippable jump. It can be built and tested separately long before the rest of Act II.

### Movement 2 — Lantern Rock (A2-01 → A2-02)

**A2-01 Arrival in the Quiet.** They emerge somewhere muted. The radio crackles silently, the dragon's roar comes out muffled, and the colors are washed out. Luke yells "HELLO?" and color ripples out from the dragon. The first lesson is that **sound brings things back**.

**A2-02 The last beacon.** The passenger leads them to Lantern Rock, a lone lighthouse still pulsing the SOS. Inside is **the Caller**, hoarse from singing.

- **Everyone understands everyone.** Luke asks how they can understand the Caller, and Maria taps the cuff of her suit. That's the whole explanation ([language](LANGUAGE_AND_TRANSLATION.md)). The Caller teaches them a greeting, *"Fair lanes!"*: hello, good luck and be safe out there.
- **The Old Song.** That night the Caller tells the old story of Morrow: the Lanemother's chosen firstborn, given the Heartnote to carry; the travelers who kept leaving; the night he held the note and a third of the roads went dark; the rest the Lanemother wrapped him in; and the garden the travelers built over him and then forgot. The full text is in the [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md) §4. It replaces the earlier bedtime story.
- The family realizes their dragon is a copy of real ones, that the real ones are held, and who is holding them.
- **The star map.** The Caller shows the song-lanes. Every lane is dark but one, leading to the Market Moon. At the far end of all of them sits a dark world: Viera, the Caller's home.
- **Lane books.** The Caller gives each family member an empty lane book and stamps the first page with Earth's mark: *"You've already been somewhere!"* Their own book is empty.
- The Caller asks for help: **wake the star-dragons, so the lanes light up and the song can start again.**
- The neighbor tries to file her complaint with the Caller. The Caller has no idea what a complaint is.
- **The Caller comes along.** With the family's noise around them, they don't need to keep singing to stay awake. They climb into the storage bay beside the neighbor. It's their first voyage.

### Movement 3 — The Lanes and the Worlds (A2-03 → A2-06)

Four worlds, each with a **sleeping star-dragon** and a stilled community. Each mirrors an Act I place, so the structure echoes ("these are their schools and stores and workshops") and young players recognize patterns. Each woken dragon lights the next lane.

**Composing the answer (owner direction, SD-020):** each world the family wakes adds one part to the SOS's answering arrangement, introduced through the world's own music and the rescue that restores it. Local people keep their own songs; the player chooses between compatible variations, never a right answer. Proposed parts for this route: **Market Moon, rhythm** (the bazaar's bells and drums); **Hatchery, harmony** (the eggs' heartbeat-songs); **Spires, lead melody** (the re-struck resonators); **Forge Ring, accents and the broadcast** (the great beacon carries the arrangement down the last lane). Act III adds the rest. There is no microphone, singing requirement, music reading or rhythm gate, and the original SOS stays recognizable.

Each world needs a landing hub and a few zones restored by sound, not one compact area. If production needs a shrink lever, a world can drop to a single hub without changing the story.

**The Sweeper across Act II:** drones carry off a sleeping cargo pod (Gravel Run), bag market stalls (Market Moon), try to box up hatchlings (escort), tow ships past the Spires and screen the Forge (Picket). At the Forge, its blueprint shows it was built there as a lane-keeper, with one rule: *clear the lanes*. Under it, newer and burning gold, Morrow's line: **"Bring everyone home."** The Barge isn't tidying. It's collecting the universe to Viera, where he can hold it all in one place.

**Introducing the Hush:** each world shows the Hush a little: a huge soft shape drifting away from noise, its place in the Old Song, its sigh in Lyla's archive. By the end of Act II the player knows it's shy, gentle and hurting, and starts to suspect who is hurting it. The full list is in [music, empathy and pacing](MUSIC_EMPATHY_AND_PACING.md) §3.

**Running gag, turning:** the neighbor's complaints get less sincere. On the Market Moon she haggles. In the Hatchery she's caught knitting a tiny scarf for a cold egg and denies it furiously. At the Spires she's the only one who goes quiet when Morrow speaks, and she doesn't say why. At the Forge she leaves the clipboard on a windowsill.

### Movement 4 — The Temptation (A2-07, the Act II ending)

With four dragons awake, the Forge's great beacon fires. The last lane lights up, longer than all the others, and runs all the way to **Viera**. On the star map, Viera sits at the end of every lane, dark, ringed by thousands of held ships, and the Sweeper's Barge is crawling toward it with more.

Then something comes back down the lane.

- **Morrow comes, as light and voice.** He can't leave Viera in body, because holding the note holds him too. Gold light fills the hangar and everything slows. The Forge Dragon flattens itself to the floor. Only the family can still move.
- **Three offers.** To Paul and Maria: stay, and the children will never grow up and leave. To the kids: stay, and it will never be bedtime. To the neighbor: stay, and it will be quiet forever, and nobody will ever touch anything again.
- **The family refuses.** Paul: *"They're supposed to grow up."* Maria: *"That's the whole point."* Lyla: *"But I want to be BIG."* Luke: *"If it's always today, I never get to be six."*
- **The neighbor accepts.** She opens the dragon's chest, and the **Bridge**, the piece that once landed in her yard, comes to her hand because it remembers her. She walks into the light. The Caller screams after her: *"He doesn't let go!"* The light withdraws down the lane, taking her and the Bridge.
- **The cost.** Without the Bridge, the dragon can't cross universes. The way home is gone, and it's in Morrow's keeping.
- **The choice, again.** Nobody asked the family to go farther. *"She's still our neighbor,"* Maria says. *"Then we go get her,"* Paul says. Luke: *"And the way home."* Luna: *"And EVERYBODY."* They choose to keep going, with no way home, as they chose in Act I with one.
- **Closing image:** the dragon at the mouth of the lane, its chest dark where the Bridge was, four star-dragons in formation behind it, turning toward Viera anyway. **Cut.**

Full draft dialogue is in the [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md) §7 and [A2-07](scripts/A2-03_WAKING_THE_WORLDS.md#a2-07--the-temptation-act-ii-ending).

*Pacing:* the Forge strike run is Act II's biggest action beat (intensity 4). The Temptation is the quietest and most frightening scene so far: no attack, only an offer. The family's choice is the relief.

## 8. Learning in Act II (proposed, follows [learning rules](../learning/LEARNING_RULES.md))

- **Real letters, numbers and words:** signs, word cards and stalls in the other universe use plain English, so every stop practices exactly what the kids are learning. The girls find letters and numerals; Luke sounds out short words ([curriculum puzzle plan](../learning/CURRICULUM_PUZZLE_PLAN.md)). Matching is recorded as matching, never as recognition or reading, and it always has an assisted path.
- **Rhythm and pattern:** re-striking resonators and hatching eggs with heartbeat-songs both involve repeating a pattern. The challenges are paused and untimed, and the hints repeat audio and narrow the choices.
- **Counting:** count hatchlings, crates and moths. Use small, tangible quantities.
- **Colors and names:** as color returns, the Core asks the child to name it. This is optional, and color is never the only answer cue.
- As in Act I, required story beats never depend on independent correctness, and help never withholds a dragon, a world or the next lane. No learning happens during flight.

## 10. Production boundaries

- **Nothing here is current scope.** [The current milestone](../plans/CURRENT_MILESTONE.md) is unchanged. Act II depends on flight, walking exploration and possibly Line Leader, none of which exist.
- The cheapest high-value slice to prototype early is **A2-00 The Crossing**, as the separately labeled flight prototype that Act I already permits.
- No multiplayer, backend or new external service is implied. The neighbor, the Caller, the passenger and the iguana are scripted scene characters, not followers or network players.
- All destination and character names are placeholders. The neighbor must stay fictional per existing rules.

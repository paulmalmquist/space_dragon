# Language and Translation

**Date:** October 30, 2026  
**Status:** The need for a way to translate language is CONFIRMED owner direction ([decision](../decisions/2026-10-30-translation.md)). The design below is PROPOSED.  
**Source:** owner, Space Dragon chat, October 30, 2026: *"We need: a way to translate language"*

The family crosses into a universe where nobody speaks English, so translation comes in three layers that grow with the story. **The Core interprets speech. The kids learn to read the writing. Lyla's visor reads songs without words.** Only one voice in that universe never needs translating, and it's Morrow's.

## 1. How Vierans talk

- **Vieran is sung.** Every word is a short melodic shape, and a sentence is a little tune. "Fair lanes!" is three rising notes.
- **Vieran writing is drawn music.** Each glyph is the shape of its word's melody, a line that rises and falls with the tune. Kids can see the sound.
- **Two glyphs matter most:**
  - **The Heartnote glyph:** a small circle with a line curving back into it, the note every song comes home to. It's the recurring symbol from the first transmission in Act I, on the Core, the pods, the dragon's chest and every lane book. This proposes a meaning for the Act I symbol, whose design is still open.
  - **Morrow's glyph:** one straight line that never ends, a single held note. In the Quiet, Lyla's visor sees it running through everything toward Viera.

## 2. Speech: the Core interprets

- **Where it comes from.** The Core was made on Viera and speaks Vieran natively. In Act I the kids teach it English, word by word ([Act I refinements](ACT_I_CRITIQUE_AND_REFINEMENTS.md) §3.2). By the full message (A1-08) it can translate in short sentences.
- **How it works.** From Act II on, the Core rides in the dragon's chest. It's linked to all five suits, which came out of the same pod, so its voice plays in every helmet, on foot or in flight. A Vieran sings a phrase; then the Core says it in English, in its own small voice.
- **No reading, ever.** Every translation is spoken. A setting can skip the sung original for younger players.
- **It starts a little wrong.** A few early lines at Lantern Rock come out funny: the Caller sings "Fair lanes!" and the Core says "Nice roads!" Lines the player needs to act on are never garbled.
- **It gets better as the kids learn.** Each word added to the Glyph Book (§4) makes the Core a little more fluent, so its Act I arc of learning from the family keeps going.
- **The Quiet wears it down.** Morrow's note thickens toward Viera, and the Core manages less: full sentences in Act II, short phrases on the road in Act III, and at Landfall, when the dragon falls asleep, the Core falls silent with it.

## 3. The Caller learns English

- Through Act II the Caller rides with the family and trades words with the kids, Luke above all: a word for a word, a running friendly contest. The Caller's English grows from single words to broken sentences.
- At Landfall, when the Core goes quiet, **the Caller becomes the family's interpreter** in Farport. The kid who sang the SOS alone now speaks for the family on their own home world.
- If the owner's Tavi is the Caller, this is Tavi and Luke's rivalry.

## 4. Writing: the Glyph Book

- **Lyla collects it.** Lyla's suit is the information-gathering suit (Act I canon). It stores every glyph the family sees in the **Glyph Book**, a page of her archive.
- **The kids decode it.** A glyph becomes known when someone matches it to its partner from home, which the Core shows beside it:
  - The girls match a glyph to a **letter or numeral** (the Find It track of the [curriculum puzzle plan](../learning/CURRICULUM_PUZZLE_PLAN.md)).
  - Luke matches a glyph-word to an **English word** (the Read It track).
- **The world becomes readable.** Once a glyph is known, its partner appears beside it everywhere, for good: signs, doors, blueprints, departure boards. The other universe becomes readable at the rate the kids learn to read.
- **Help is always there.** The Core, or later the Caller, can read any glyph aloud. That's recorded as assisted, and the story never waits on it.
- **Where it pays off.** In Farport, with the Core silent, the family reads its own way through: the Big Board, the shop signs on Outfitters' Row, the Rest Garden gate. What the kids learned in Acts I and II becomes the way forward.
- **Story glyphs:** *hello*, *friend*, *home* and *rest* (the drafted waking beats), the Heartnote, and Morrow's name. At the Forge, the Core won't read the name on the oldest blueprint. Luke sounds it out himself: *"Mor… row. Morrow."* Reading becomes courage.

## 5. Songs without words: Lyla listens

- Star-dragons, hatchlings and creatures don't use words; they sing. Lyla's visor turns their songs into pictures, with no reading: *a nest*, *follow me*, *danger*, *hungry*, *thank you*.
- The passenger's chirps are the first entries. In the owner's character document this is Lyla's dictionary of Mox's chirps.
- It's how the family understands the star-dragons they wake, and the Lanemother when she first opens her eyes.

## 6. The voice that needs no translation

- **Morrow speaks every language.** His note runs through every song in the universe, so he speaks to each person in their own words. To the family he speaks warm, perfect English, the first perfect English they've heard since leaving Earth.
- **The Core goes silent whenever he speaks.** It's afraid of him.
- At the Spires, Luke notices:

> LUKE: "Why does he talk like us?"
>
> CALLER: "He talks like everybody."

- **The gift comes back.** When Morrow lets go of the Heartnote at the Last Hold, his gift of tongues goes back into the song with it. From then on everyone understands everyone: the Lanemother speaks, and each person hears her in their own words. Her first words the family understands without help are to Paul and Maria at the final landing. The Core, freed from translating, gets to just talk.

## 7. Rules for play

- No reading is required anywhere. Every translation is spoken, and Glyph Book labels are extra help.
- Known glyphs and words are saved and never lost.
- Mistranslations are jokes, never wrong directions.
- The Core has one consistent character voice, a placeholder until it's cast. No voice cloning, per the repository rules.
- Glyph Book stops follow the [learning rules](../learning/LEARNING_RULES.md): matching is recorded as matching, never as letter-name recognition or reading.

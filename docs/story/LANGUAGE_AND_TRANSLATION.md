# Language and Translation

**Date:** October 30, 2026  
**Status:** CONFIRMED owner direction ([decision](../decisions/2026-10-30-translation.md)): the story needs a way to translate language, and **the suits can translate**. The details below are PROPOSED.  
**Source:** owner, Space Dragon chat, October 30, 2026: *"We need: a way to translate language"* and *"The suits can translate"*

The family crosses into a universe where nobody speaks English, and **their suits translate.** The suits are living star-dragon scale, the same material the song runs through, and they've been listening to the family since the night they formed in the backyard. So they understand what's sung around them and say it in their wearer's ear, and they sing the family's words back out in Vieran. The one thing the suits can't do is read: they translate what they hear, not what they see. Writing is the kids' job. Only one voice in that universe never needs translating, and it's Morrow's.

## 1. How Vierans talk

- **Vieran is sung.** Every word is a short melodic shape, and a sentence is a little tune. "Fair lanes!" is three rising notes.
- **Vieran writing is drawn music.** Each glyph is the shape of its word's melody, a line that rises and falls with the tune. Kids can see the sound.
- **Two glyphs matter most:**
  - **The Heartnote glyph:** a small circle with a line curving back into it, the note every song comes home to. It's the recurring symbol from the first transmission in Act I, on the Core, the pods, the dragon's chest and every lane book. This proposes a meaning for the Act I symbol, whose design is still open.
  - **Morrow's glyph:** one straight line that never ends, a single held note. In the Quiet, Lyla's visor sees it running through everything toward Viera.

## 2. Speech: the suits translate

- **Why they can.** The suits are grown from shed star-dragon scale, which carries the song, and Vieran *is* song. They formed around the family in Act I by reading what each person was doing ([Act I refinements](ACT_I_CRITIQUE_AND_REFINEMENTS.md) §3.3), and they've been listening to the family ever since, the same way the Core learned its words. By the time the family crosses, every suit knows English and Vieran both.
- **Listening.** When a Vieran sings, each suit says it in English in its wearer's ear, quietly, a beat behind. Helmets don't need to be closed; the suit hums it close.
- **Speaking.** When a family member talks, their suit sings their words out in Vieran, in **their own instrument voice**: Paul's words come out brassy, Luna's bounce like a marimba, Lyla's chime. Vierans hear the family singing. The family hears themselves talking. It's one of the game's small delights, and it ties translation to the [music system](MUSIC_EMPATHY_AND_PACING.md) §2.2.
- **The neighbor has no suit.** Her robot iguana does the job. It has a little living material inside it from the Bridge, so it translates into her ear from her shoulder, in a tinny, flat, slightly sarcastic voice. When she goes to Morrow at the Forge, she doesn't need it: he speaks her language. The iguana stays with the family.
- **No reading, ever.** Every translation is spoken. A setting can skip the sung original for younger players.
- **It switches on at the crossing.** In Act I there's nobody to translate; the Core does the talking (A1-08). The first time the suits translate is at Lantern Rock, and it surprises everyone.
- **It starts a little wrong.** The suits learned their English from a family, not a dictionary. A few early lines at Lantern Rock come out funny: the Caller sings "Fair lanes!" and five suits whisper "Nice roads!" Lines the player needs to act on are never garbled.
- **It gets better as the kids learn.** Each word added to the Glyph Book (§4) sharpens the suits a little.
- **The Quiet wears it down.** The suits are made of Morrow's song, so they dim as his note thickens toward Viera. Their translating stays whole in Act II, starts dropping the odd word on the road in Act III, and in Farport leaves gaps: *"Who ___ these? They're half ___."* The suits never go silent; they just lose words.

## 3. The Caller fills the gaps

- Through Act II the Caller rides with the family and trades words with the kids, Luke above all: a word for a word, a running friendly contest. They don't *need* to, because the suits translate. They do it because they're friends.
- It pays off in Farport. When the suits start dropping words, **the Caller fills the gaps**, in careful, broken English. The kid who sang the SOS alone now speaks for the family on their own home world.
- If the owner's Tavi is the Caller, this is Tavi and Luke's rivalry.

## 4. Writing: the Glyph Book

- **The suits can't read.** They hear; they don't see. Vieran writing is drawn, so it stays a mystery until someone learns it.
- **Lyla collects it.** Lyla's suit is the information-gathering suit (Act I canon). Her visor stores every glyph the family sees in the **Glyph Book**, a page of her archive.
- **The kids decode it.** A glyph becomes known when someone matches it to its partner from home:
  - The girls match a glyph to a **letter or numeral** (the Find It track of the [curriculum puzzle plan](../learning/CURRICULUM_PUZZLE_PLAN.md)).
  - Luke matches a glyph-word to an **English word** (the Read It track).
- **The world becomes readable.** Once a glyph is known, its partner appears beside it everywhere, for good: signs, doors, blueprints, departure boards. The other universe becomes readable at the rate the kids learn to read.
- **Help is always there.** The Core, or later the Caller, can read any glyph aloud. That's recorded as assisted, and the story never waits on it.
- **Where it pays off.** In Farport, with the suits dropping words and the Core asleep in the dragon, the family reads its own way through: the Big Board, the shop signs on Outfitters' Row, the Rest Garden gate. What the kids learned in Acts I and II becomes the way forward.
- **Story glyphs:** *hello*, *friend*, *home* and *rest* (the drafted waking beats), the Heartnote, and Morrow's name. At the Forge, the Core won't read the name on the oldest blueprint, and the suits can't. Luke sounds it out himself: *"Mor… row. Morrow."* Reading becomes courage.

## 5. Songs without words: Lyla listens

- Star-dragons, hatchlings and creatures don't use words; they sing music without language, so even the suits only catch the feeling. Lyla's visor turns those songs into pictures, with no reading: *a nest*, *follow me*, *danger*, *hungry*, *thank you*.
- The passenger's chirps are the first entries. In the owner's character document this is Lyla's dictionary of Mox's chirps.
- It's how the family understands the star-dragons they wake.

## 6. The voice that needs no translation

- **Morrow speaks every language.** His note runs through every song in the universe, so he speaks to each person in their own words. To the family he speaks warm, perfect English, the first voice in the other universe the suits don't have to touch.
- **The suits shiver when he speaks.** They're made of his song, and they know their oldest voice. Their glow flickers, and for as long as he talks, they don't say anything.
- At the Spires, Luke notices:

> LUKE: "Why does he talk like us?"
>
> CALLER: "He talks like everybody."

- **The Lanemother speaks Vieran.** Through the Last Hold, the suits translate her for the family, softly, in five voices at once.
- **The gift comes back.** When Morrow lets go of the Heartnote at the Last Hold, his gift of tongues goes back into the song with it. From then on everyone understands everyone, with or without a suit. Luke pops his helmet open and still understands the Lanemother. Her first words the family hears with no translation at all are to Paul and Maria at the final landing.

## 7. Rules for play

- No reading is required anywhere. Every translation is spoken, and Glyph Book labels are extra help.
- Known glyphs and words are saved and never lost.
- Mistranslations and dropped words are jokes or texture, never wrong directions. The Caller or a hint always fills a gap the player needs.
- Translation is a passive suit ability with no button and no meter. It never costs anything and can't be lost.
- Each suit's translated voice is its wearer's instrument voice, a placeholder until voices are cast. No voice cloning, per the repository rules.
- Glyph Book stops follow the [learning rules](../learning/LEARNING_RULES.md): matching is recorded as matching, never as letter-name recognition or reading.

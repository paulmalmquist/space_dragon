## Viera, the lanes and the Quiet

Viera is a planet where everyone is a space traveler, and the Quiet started there because its travelers forgot to come home and rest. Names in this section are placeholders.

### Viera

- **People.** Every Vieran is a traveler. Each one carries a **lane book** stamped with a mark from every world they've reached. A child earns their first mark on their first voyage.
- **Words.** Vierans greet each other with "Fair lanes!" and part with "Light your way home."
- **Farport** is the capital, a spaceport city of landing fields, outfitters, enormous departure halls and dragon roosts.
- **The Mooring Ring** circles the planet: thousands of traveler ships docked side by side, like a harbor in the sky.
- **The Rest Garden** sits at Farport's heart. Travelers used to sit there before a long trip. Nobody has stopped there in a very long time.

### The song-lanes

Star-dragons sing **song-lanes** between worlds, and ships ride them like ocean currents. The oldest star-dragon, **the Lanemother**, sang the first lane, and every lane ends at Viera.

This is why the route is linear. A lane only lights up when the dragon at its far end is awake, so each dragon the family wakes opens the way to the next world.

### Why the Quiet happened

Every song in this universe comes home to one note, **the Heartnote**. The Lanemother gave it to her firstborn, **Morrow**, to carry, so travelers could always find their way back. When the Vierans kept going farther and coming home less, Morrow couldn't bear it. He closed his jaws on the Heartnote and **held** it, and the whole song stopped. That held note is the Quiet. See the [villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md).

Long ago the Lanemother bound him in a rest, the Hush, and the travelers built the Rest Garden over him and rested there before every journey to keep the seal strong. They loved traveling so much that they stopped resting, and forgot why. The seal cracked, and he woke. The travelers' forgetting let him out; he is the reason for the SOS.

### Rules of the Quiet

- Living things inside it are held mid-moment, glowing faintly and unharmed. That includes people, star-dragons and the living material.
- **Machines keep running.** That's why the Sweeper never stopped.
- Only someone whose Heartnote lives outside this universe can move and make sound inside it. That's why the SOS left the universe.

### How the SOS was sent

As the Quiet spread from Viera, the Forge Ring's shipwrights finished an old dragon design. They packed it with a Core and five suits, one for each seat in a star-dragon pod, and sent it through a short-lived opening. Then the Forge fell asleep.

The Caller kept the SOS going from Lantern Rock, the last place the Quiet reached, by singing it without stopping. Earth was in range.

## The Sweeper

The Sweeper is a Vieran lane-keeper robot. Machines can't be held, so Morrow uses it as his hands: it has been hauling every held ship in the universe to Viera. It gives the dragon a real fleet to dogfight across two acts without anyone getting hurt.

- **What it is.** A barge-sized robot built at the Forge Ring to clear rocks and wreckage out of the lanes. It's polite, tireless and very proud of its work. It announces itself with a cheerful "TIDYING!" (placeholder line).
- **Why it's trouble.** Its old rule was *clear the lanes*. Under it, Morrow burned a new one: **"Bring everyone home."** It has been collecting the held ships, stacking them at its depot on the Waystation and towing them to the Mooring Ring.
- **Its fleet.** Unmanned drones: fast fighters, grapplers, gunships, an interdictor net and the Barge itself. Details are in the flight section.
- **On foot:** Tidy-bots that try to bag anything left lying around, and the Crane, its depot foreman.

### Its arc

1. **Act II, sightings.** Drones carry off a sleeping cargo pod, bag market stalls and try to box up floating hatchling eggs.
2. **Act II, the reveal.** At the Forge Ring, Lyla finds the Sweeper's blueprint hanging beside the dragon's own, with Morrow's rule burning under the old one.
3. **Act II, the cliffhanger.** The Barge is heading for Viera with more ships for his collection.
4. **Act III, the chase.** The family frees ships at its depot, outflies its drones in the Drift and fights the Barge above Viera.
5. **Act III, the re-tune.** At the Battle of the Mooring Ring, with the Barge crippled, the robot iguana talks to its core first, and the Caller gives it the rule Morrow's rewrite buried: *some things that are still are only resting.* It's never destroyed.
6. **Act III, the turn.** It flies with the family in the Last Hold, towing freed ships clear, and becomes Farport's harbor tug.

**Why it fits:** like the neighbor, it only wanted things tidy and quiet, and Morrow used that. The Barge was never tidying. It was collecting the universe to Viera for him.

## Act II — Answer the Call

Act II keeps the drafted worlds in a fixed order, adds a flight level on every lane between them, and ends at the Forge Ring with Viera in sight. *Question: how do we help?*

1. **The Crossing.** As drafted: Earth orbit, light speed, the ring, and the stowaway neighbor found in the storage bay.
2. **Lantern Rock.** Arrival in the Quiet and meeting the Caller, as drafted. New: the Caller opens the star map, and the family sees Viera, dark at the center. Only one lane glows, to the Market Moon.
3. **Lane: the Gravel Run.** An asteroid-field chase after a Sweeper drone carrying a sleeping cargo pod. The family's first look at the Sweeper.
4. **Market Moon** (Paul and Luna). As drafted, plus Tidy-bots bagging the sleeping merchants' stalls. The Market Dragon wakes (1 of 4) and gifts scales to Paul and Luna.
5. **Lane: the Fog.** A blind run through a dim nebula, where the dragon's roar lights up the rocks like sonar.
6. **Hatchery** (Maria and Lyla). As drafted, now a warm planet inside a nebula. The passenger finds its family, the emotional midpoint. The Hatchery Dragon wakes (2 of 4) and gifts scales to Maria and Lyla.
7. **Lane: Hatchling escort, then the Shardstorm.** Protect the hatchlings from grappler drones trying to box them up. Then race through a belt of crystal shards that ring like bells when you clip them.
8. **Climbing Spires** (Luke). As drafted. The Spire Dragon wakes (3 of 4) and gifts a scale to Luke. From the top, Luke sees the Barge towing a long chain of sleeping ships.
9. **Lane: the Sweeper Picket.** The first full squadron dogfight, plus a slow, shielded gunship.
10. **Forge Ring** (everyone). As drafted: the five-suit set piece, the dragon's blueprints, Song-breath and the trench run. New: Luke reads Morrow's name on the blueprints; the family's dragon is his likeness. The Sweeper's blueprint shows Morrow's rewritten rule. The Forge Dragon wakes (4 of 4).

**Act II ending: the Temptation.** The Forge's great beacon fires, and the last lane lights up all the way to Viera. Then Morrow comes back down it, as light and voice, and makes his offer: to the parents, children who never grow up and leave; to the kids, a today that never ends; to the neighbor, quiet forever. The family refuses. The neighbor accepts, takes the Bridge from the dragon's chest, and goes to him. With no way home, the family chooses to go on.

> LUNA: "And EVERYBODY."

**What carries over unchanged:** the waking beats keyed to "dragons awake" now just happen in this order. The word cards (HELLO, FRIEND, HOME, REST), the Caller's growing theme and the neighbor's clipboard gag stay as written.

## Act III — The Road to Viera

Act III races the Sweeper to Viera, puts the drafted Hush climax in Farport's Rest Garden, and ends with powers, a fleet-sized final battle and the final landing. *Question: where do travelers belong?* The answer: even travelers need a home to come back to.

### On the way

1. **Lane: the Snag Net.** Mid-jump, the Sweeper's interdictor net yanks the dragon out of light speed. Dogfight the guard drones and cut the net's four anchors to break free.
2. **The Waystation.** A ringed moon that used to be the travelers' rest stop: a big mess hall, hangars and a house band asleep mid-song. The Sweeper turned it into its depot, with sleeping ships stacked like a junkyard.
    - On foot: the family's instruments wake the house band, and the level's music comes back with them.
    - Free the stacked ships, then beat **the Crane**, the depot's foreman bot (on-foot boss).
    - In the Crane's office, a crate holds the neighbor's old clipboard, collected and filed. The robot iguana won't leave it behind.
    - The dragon learns **Tow-line**, a tether of light for towing ships.
3. **The Drift.** A huge asteroid field around Viera's moon, full of ships that tumbled when their pilots fell asleep.
    - Thread the field, dock with sleeping ships, and wake their pilots with a short on-foot boarding scene.
    - The woken star-dragons tow the rescued ships in a growing convoy behind you.
4. **Battle of the Mooring Ring.** Squadron dogfights above Viera, then a trench run along the Barge to disable its grapple arms. Freed ships drift back to their moorings. The Barge limps away into the clouds.

### Landfall

5. **The low point.** The dragon gave everything in the battle. Deep in the Quiet, it falls asleep and glides down onto a Farport landing field, then curls up. The drafted low-point beat moves here: dark, quiet, the family close together, and Lyla's home recording pushing the color back.
6. **Farport on foot.** The biggest world in the game, crossed in a set order:
    - **Landing Fields:** sleeping ships and the curled-up dragon. Maria keeps it breathing softly.
    - **Outfitters' Row:** the family wakes the outfitters, who upgrade each suit so it can go deeper into the Quiet (see Suits to powers).
    - **The Departure Halls:** a giant terminal with travelers asleep mid-goodbye, suitcases floating.
    - **The Roosts:** star-dragon nests, and the path to the center.
    - **The Rest Garden:** overgrown, and the place where Morrow lies coiled over his old seal.

### The center

7. **Listen, Shelter, Heartnote.** Morrow lies coiled over the broken seal, holding his own mother still, with the Hush crushed beneath him and the neighbor in her lawn chair at his side. The neighbor hears the Hush crying, stands up out of his hold, and sits with it: "I know that feeling." The family shelters the Hush from shards of his note, and the neighbor returns the Bridge. Then they sing Earth's Heartnote, followed by a rest, and a held note can't survive a rest. His note breaks. Their dragon wakes, the Lanemother wakes, Viera wakes, and Morrow opens his eyes.
8. **Your Own Note.** As Viera wakes, each suit's living material hears its wearer's own note and blooms. Every upgrade fuses into **one power each**.

### The finale

9. **The Great Launch and the Last Hold.** Every traveler on Viera wakes and takes off at once, a sky full of ships leaving, the thing Morrow feared most. He stands up for the first time. *"STAY."*
    - **Phase 1, the sky:** he hurls gold Holds at the fleeing ships; the dragon leads every star-dragon, the hatchlings, the re-tuned Sweeper and every traveler ship, breaking Holds with Song-breath.
    - **Phase 2, his back:** the dragon lands on him, and the family uses their new powers to climb to the stolen Heartnote in his throat.
    - **Phase 3, the choice:** nobody strikes. Lyla plays him the Old Song, Luke offers him a way out, and the Lanemother calls him by his name. He lets go of the Heartnote, can no longer move, and becomes **the Lodestar**, the fixed star travelers steer home by.
10. **The Final Landing.** The whole fleet lands at Farport in formation.
    - Every family member's lane book gets stamped with Viera's mark. The Caller gets their very first mark.
    - The dragon is home among its makers, under the star of the dragon it was modeled on. It looks at them, then lowers its wing for the family.
    - The Bridge goes back into its chest, and the Lanemother sings a lane home. To Paul and Maria: *"Hold them. And then let them go. Both. That is the whole song."*
    - The neighbor's folding chair goes in the Rest Garden. The Vierans start sitting in it.
    - Closing image: Farport at night, travelers resting in the garden, and in the sky the Lodestar, with the lane home to Earth glowing steady beside it.

**Post-credits:** the cul-de-sac block party. A small Vieran ship lands on the street. It's the Caller, on their second voyage, with a cake-sized gift for the baker neighbor.

## Suits to powers

The suits grow in three steps: small upgrades from each star-dragon, a big upgrade from Farport's outfitters, then one power each when Viera wakes. Upgrades are things a suit **has**: new parts you can see. Powers are things a person **can do**: the living material and its wearer finally singing the same note.

### The three steps

1. **Scale gifts (Act II).** Each woken star-dragon sheds a scale for the family members featured on its world. The suit absorbs it and gains one small upgrade.
2. **Outfitters' Row (Act III, on arrival in Farport).** The woken outfitters give each suit one big upgrade that lets it go deeper into the Quiet. This is the main upgrade moment.
3. **Your Own Note (Act III, the end).** At the Heartnote, each suit hears its wearer's note and blooms. Its upgrades fuse into one signature power, used straight away in the Great Launch.

### Rules that keep it right for the family

- The three-year-olds' powers are as big and as fun as anyone's.
- Every power sounds in its owner's voice, which morphs from instrument into synth as the power arrives (owner direction, SD-022), so the finale is a song.
- Five Together becomes **Five Powers**, and its chord finally resolves to the Heartnote, as the [music doc](MUSIC_EMPATHY_AND_PACING.md) already planned.
- The living-material guardrails hold: powers always switch off, helmets always open, and the suit never controls its wearer.
- No power ever replaces a learning challenge or makes one required.

### Room left on purpose

Each suit has one more open upgrade slot at Farport, and the dragon has an open slot at the Dragonwrights. A second power per person is held back for a post-game or sequel.

## Smaller fixes

Three small threads tie the acts together: Viera seeded on Earth, lane books that fill up as you travel, and one learning stop per Act III world.

### Seed Viera in Act I

- The recurring symbol from the first transmission becomes the **Vieran travelers' mark**. It's on the Core, the pods, the Caller's lighthouse and every lane book.
- The full message (A1-08) ends on one new image: a planet ringed with thousands of ships. Nobody knows what it is yet.
- Lock the Act I mission order: preschool, Luke's school, warehouse store, grocery, big-box store, rocket facility.

### Lane books

- At Lantern Rock the Caller gives each family member an empty lane book, then stamps the first page with Earth's mark. "You've already been somewhere!"
- Every world adds a stamp: Market Moon, Hatchery, Spires, Forge Ring, Waystation, the Drift.
- Optional discoveries on each world add bonus stamps, so big worlds have something to find.
- The last page is Viera, stamped at the final landing alongside the Caller's very first mark.

# Space Dragon — Planet Missions and the Growing SOS

**Date:** October 29, 2026  
**User direction:** The adventure should evolve into planet-to-planet missions in which the family composes the SOS.  
**Status:** PROPOSED campaign development implementing that direction. World names, mission details, music mechanics, dialogue, and new lore below remain proposals. This file does not change repository canon or establish implementation scope.  
**Companion:** `SPACE_DRAGON_NEW_CHARACTERS_AND_STORY_BEATS.md`

## 1. The campaign spine

**Build the dragon on Earth. Build the song across the stars.**

The family understands the incoming SOS before leaving Earth. Their town missions still recover the equipment, repair the message, assemble the dragon, and let them choose to answer it. The planetary campaign must not postpone the reason for their journey or turn their accidental arrival into destiny.

At the Last Beacon, Tavi explains the next problem. The small call that reached Earth needs to become a larger rescue signal: one that reconnects the sleeping worlds and carries their voices and locations to one another. The surviving SOS melody is its recognizable seed. The family and the people they help compose the complete piece together.

There is no hidden master recording to collect. Each world contributes music that could not exist in quite this form without the mission that just happened. A repaired market contributes its rhythm; living roots contribute a bass voice; a reunion contributes a harmony. Local people remain collaborators, not sources from whom the family takes musical trophies.

The growing song has two successive purposes:

1. **Reach and reconnect.** It helps restored worlds find one another, coordinate rescues, and establish a path to the silent center.
2. **Make room for everyone.** At the center, the family discovers that a stronger broadcast cannot solve the Hush's distress. They rearrange the same music into a conversation with pauses, gentler entries, and a final home note.

The SOS changes from one child's plea into many worlds answering one another.

## 2. What composing means in play

Composition happens mainly through the adventure, not through compulsory music-editor screens.

- **Discover a voice:** hear a damaged, muted, or incomplete musical idea in the environment.
- **Help its source:** complete a rescue, traversal challenge, encounter, or repair that lets the source speak again.
- **Shape its contribution:** use a suit ability on a physical instrument or select between a few demonstrated, compatible musical phrases. This is a creative choice, not a correct-answer test.
- **Hear the consequence:** the world's contribution joins the song, changes the local soundscape, and remains audible aboard the dragon and during later travel.

Lyla records, compares, and arranges the contributions. She does not steal anyone's voice. The local inhabitants join willingly, and their music remains on their world after the family leaves.

A simple song constellation aboard the dragon lets players hear their work: select a world to feature its voice, compare the original little SOS with the current arrangement, or revisit a musical choice. This is optional. Assisted defaults always produce a coherent score and never block the story.

The player shapes the arrangement, while the game keeps pitches and timing compatible. Movement, combat, and rescues respond immediately; soundtrack synchronization must not delay a dodge, parry, web shot, or healing action. No microphone, real singing, music reading, or precise rhythm performance is required.

## 3. Opening mission — The Last Beacon: “Somebody Answered”

**Purpose:** Answer the initial mystery immediately and establish why the planets matter.

The dragon reaches the source of the SOS. Tavi is there, standing on books to reach the controls, maintaining a distress call that is no longer strong enough to reach the nearest worlds.

The first task is to steady the failing beacon together. Luke webs a swaying support, Dad braces its housing, Maria restores its living material, Luna carries a replacement component along a goo slide, and Lyla preserves Tavi's melody before the speaker falls quiet. These are sequential contextual contributions, not five simultaneous controls.

Tavi expects a professional rescue crew. Instead she gets the family, a pink trail across the floor, and Mox repeating Dad's voice.

> **Tavi:** “You heard it?”  
> **Luke:** “It interrupted Dad's computer.”  
> **Tavi:** “I wasn't sure it reached anyone.”

The family restores enough local sound to let Tavi rest. The Core reveals the routes to the first worlds. This is not another hunt to learn what the SOS means: they already know people need help.

**Starting music:** Tavi's small, exposed SOS phrase. Every later arrangement preserves a recognizable version of it.

## 4. World One — The Market Moon: “Get the World Moving”

**Mission identity:** Moving rescue, cart chase, first lively settlement.  
**Featured family:** Dad and Luna, with a meaningful contribution from everyone.  
**Character development:** Meet Captain Nera.  
**Musical contribution:** The beat.

The market is built around a circular transport system. When its song stopped, bridges stalled, stalls tilted, and cargo carts locked together across the square. The merchants are asleep beside the things they had been carrying.

The family first makes a safe pocket of sound. They wake Nera near her stranded cargo ship and discover that its blankets and supplies were intended for sleeping communities farther away. She wants to evacuate whoever they can reach. The family wants to restore the routes so nobody has to be left behind.

### Playable sequence

1. Explore the stalled square and locate the people beneath an overturned stall.
2. Dad lifts the stall while Maria restores the exhausted inhabitants. Lyla finds a second access route. Luke anchors a cart. Luna moves supplies through the narrow opening on a controlled goo slide.
3. Restart the market's transport route and ride a cart through the district, disabling malfunctioning capture drones and clearing obstacles.
4. Redirect moving carts through large resonant gates. Their wheels, bells, and cargo drums become a rhythm section.

This should feel like a rescue that becomes a joyous chase, not like standing at a row of switches.

**Composition choice:** The returning merchants demonstrate two compatible grooves: a bouncing hand-drum pattern or a rolling metallic pattern. The player's route through the gates shapes which one becomes prominent. Either rescues the market.

**Payoff:** The Market Dragon wakes. The transport system moves safely, stalls reopen, and residents play along. The song now has a pulse. Nera offers a route onward and helps with deliveries, although she is not yet convinced the family can restore everything.

> **Luna:** “We made it dance.”  
> **Dad:** “We were trying to move the groceries.”

## 5. World Two — Whisperwood: “Find the Heartbeat”

**Mission identity:** Exploration, navigation, living-environment repair.  
**Featured family:** Maria and Lyla.  
**Character development:** Establish that listening is useful before it becomes the final answer.  
**Musical contribution:** Bass and a sustained foundation.

Whisperwood's enormous trees once carried low notes through their roots. With the forest asleep, paths have folded inward and branches have curled around the sleeping root-dragon. A loud roar wakes a few leaves but does not reveal how the forest fits together.

Lyla detects a faint pulse beneath the ground. It is too quiet to notice while everyone is stomping around.

### Playable sequence

1. Follow visual ripples and gentle vibrations to map the buried root network. Sound is reinforced by readable visual cues.
2. Heal knotted sections with Maria, opening routes rather than merely filling a health bar.
3. Use the family's abilities to redirect water, brace a root arch, and reach a sheltered grove. The party stays together.
4. Guide the returning pulses into the great root chambers so the forest's low voice can travel again.

**Composition choice:** Help the root chambers sustain a long, warm bass note or answer one another with a gentle repeating pulse. Both fit the established SOS.

**Payoff:** The root-dragon uncurls and the forest opens. The song acquires depth beneath its market rhythm.

**Early Hush clue:** Lyla records a small shudder between root pulses. The Core initially classifies it as interference. She keeps the recording anyway.

> **Maria:** “Wait. Don't make a sound.”  
> **Lyla:** “There. It was already singing.”

## 6. World Three — The Climbing Spires: “Strings Across the Sky”

**Mission identity:** Vertical traversal, rivalry, first Rook encounter.  
**Featured family:** Luke.  
**Character development:** Luke and Tavi learn to trust one another; Rook becomes a recurring obstacle.  
**Musical contribution:** A developed lead melody.

The planet's crystal towers are connected by enormous resonating lines. They once carried the SOS motif across the sky. Several lines have snapped, leaving bridges hanging and the upper towers isolated.

Luke's web power becomes the heart of the mission: swing between anchors, catch moving platforms, and temporarily replace missing resonator lines. This is an active power, not just an automatic safety catch.

### Playable sequence

1. Reach the first suspended district through a readable climbing and swinging route.
2. Tavi proposes an interior shortcut; Luke spots an exterior route. Their competition brings them to the same loose platform.
3. Luke catches the platform with a web, but Tavi identifies the anchor strong enough to hold it. Their first real teamwork opens the next route.
4. Restoring the station wakes Rook, who encloses the landing area for everyone's supposed protection.
5. Escape a containment encounter: avoid or optionally parry his staff, use goo to disrupt capture drones, and web his shield mechanisms without destroying him.
6. Reconnect the high resonators and let the wind play the temporary web-lines.

**Composition choice:** Luke can arrange the safe resonator connections into one of several guided melodic shapes. The signature SOS remains recognizable, while its answering phrase reflects the player's choices.

**Payoff:** The Spire Dragon wakes and the song carries far beyond the local world. Luke has made a literal bridge and a musical one. Rook withdraws to prepare a safer, more elaborate capture attempt.

> **Tavi:** “That is not what webs are for.”  
> **Luke:** “They are now.”

## 7. World Four — The Hatchery Nebula: “Bring Everybody Home”

**Mission identity:** Gentle rescue and escort; emotional midpoint.  
**Featured family:** Maria and Lyla, with Luna and Mox central to the action.  
**Character development:** Mox finds his family.  
**Musical contribution:** Harmony and responding voices.

This destination is a nursery world inside the Hatchery Nebula. Its floating nest-islands have drifted apart. Keepers and many of the parent creatures are asleep; the eggs still carry tiny, faint heartbeat-songs.

The family is not collecting eggs. They are warming their shelters, reuniting displaced hatchlings with their proper nests, and helping the local keepers resume their work.

### Playable sequence

1. Wake a keeper and restore one safe nesting area.
2. Maria restores the living nests while Lyla distinguishes the heartbeat patterns inside them.
3. Move between nearby islands with the whole party. Luna builds soft slide paths and Luke secures small floating platforms for the hatchlings.
4. Match responding calls to reunite hatchlings and keepers. Matching is supported, visual as well as audible, and never a progress gate.
5. Follow the familiar three-note call and clicking sound that Mox has made since Earth.

A newly awakened enormous creature answers. Mox becomes still for the first time in the adventure, then runs straight toward her.

It is his mother.

Lyla had recorded Mox's call because it mattered to him, before anyone knew it would be useful.

**Composition choice:** The reunited families offer a soft humming harmony or a playful call-and-response. Both can later be arranged gently at the center.

**Payoff:** The nursery dragon wakes; parents, keepers, and hatchlings answer the SOS together. The song finally sounds as though someone is responding to Tavi. Mox stays for a quiet reunion, then chooses to continue with the family after his mother recognizes their purpose. Their separation is voluntary, affectionate, and clearly safe.

No compulsory farewell timer, cold-egg countdown, or distressing failure state belongs here.

## 8. World Five — Tideglass: “The Song Under the Sea”

**Mission identity:** Underwater exploration and a spatial sound puzzle.  
**Featured family:** Luna and Lyla.  
**Character development:** Listening becomes perspective-taking rather than simply detecting a clue.  
**Musical contribution:** A countermelody that answers instead of competing.

Tideglass has shallow, luminous seas above a city of bell-shaped towers. Its quiet signal network is trapped in disconnected chambers beneath the water. From above, the place appears empty. From inside the chambers, tiny answering lights are visible.

The family does not need to shout harder. They need to carry sound to the places it cannot yet reach.

### Playable sequence

1. Fly low over the water, using visible sound ripples to locate a safe landing and submerged entrance.
2. Explore enclosed, bright underwater routes with the family together. Suits provide protection without oxygen meters or drowning hazards.
3. Use Luna's goo to seal gaps temporarily, allowing trapped air bubbles to move through the sound chambers.
4. Dad rotates a bell housing, Luke holds a passage open, Maria restores its living lining, and Lyla maps which chambers answer one another.
5. Lead the bubbles along different paths. Their passage plays the bell-city's answering phrase.

**Composition choice:** Choose which compatible response follows the main melody: a rising, curious phrase or a descending, soothing one. There is no single correct emotional answer.

**Payoff:** The Tide Dragon wakes, and the city rises enough to reconnect its residents with the surface. A previously missing countermelody joins the SOS.

**Hush clue:** In one chamber Lyla hears the same shudder recorded in Whisperwood, now clearer. It seems to be a breath rather than broken machinery. The family still does not know who is making it.

## 9. World Six — The Forge Ring: “The Guardian's Choice”

**Mission identity:** Major dragon-flight encounter, on-foot action, rescue reversal.  
**Featured family:** Dad and Luke in combat, Lyla in discovery, everyone in the rescue.  
**Character development:** Rook chooses to help; Nera commits to the collective rescue.  
**Musical contribution:** Accents, brass-like power, and the ability to broadcast the ensemble.

The Forge Ring is the orbital shipyard where the rescue dragon's design originated. Its relay can carry the growing SOS between the restored worlds. Its security systems, however, are still enforcing a lockdown intended to protect the inhabitants.

### Playable sequence

1. Pilot the dragon through the ring while avoiding or disabling unmanned containment craft. The dragon uses one pilot, not separate family crew stations.
2. Land inside a compact forge district and reach the relay through disabled machinery.
3. Encounter Rook again. His shield, staff, and capture units create opportunities for dodges, optional precision parries, and family alley-oops.
4. Lyla finds old designs showing that the dragon and its crew configuration existed long before this family. There is no prophecy or secret selection.
5. Rook's lockdown mechanism jams and traps him while the station begins closing around its own rescue equipment.
6. The family turns back. Dad braces the closing structure, Maria restores damaged living material, Luna clears a sliding route, Luke tethers Rook, and Lyla identifies the release sequence.

The rescue is not a hacking trick that instantly changes Rook's personality. Their actions give him reason to reconsider what protection requires.

> **Rook:** “You returned.”  
> **Luke:** “You were still in there.”

**Composition choice:** Use the repaired forge's resonant machinery to choose where the song's strong accents land. The players are shaping emphasis, not merely adding another permanently loud layer.

**Payoff:** The Forge Dragon wakes. Rook helps open the relay, and Nera coordinates transport between the worlds. The complete ensemble broadcasts for the first time. Previously isolated settlements begin answering each other. The SOS has achieved a real rescue function before the final confrontation.

Then Mox imitates the small, shuddering breath. Lyla overlays her recordings from different worlds. Maria hears it clearly:

> **Maria:** “That sounds like someone trying not to cry.”

## 10. Final World — Stillheart: “Leave Room for Quiet”

**Mission identity:** Gentle low point, understanding, protective action, final arrangement.  
**Featured family:** Everyone; Lyla's recordings connect the evidence and the neighbor's empathy matters.  
**Character development:** The family learns that the successful answer to one problem is not automatically the answer to another.  
**Musical contribution:** Rests, turn-taking, and the final home note.

The growing SOS opens a safe route toward the sleeping Elder Dragon. But as the family approaches the center, their own living dragon grows drowsy and settles gently onto the ground. The suits dim. Nobody is separated, and nobody dies or is abandoned.

Lyla plays a small recording from home. Familiar voices and ordinary household sounds give the family a little circle of color to walk inside.

They find the Hush curled around the Elder Dragon, overwhelmed by every world's sound arriving at once. Playing the triumphant full ensemble makes it withdraw further. The song has reconnected the worlds, but the arrangement is wrong for this encounter.

### Phase 1 — Listen

Lyla compares the recurring breath with the Hush's reactions. Her suit briefly makes its perspective understandable: too many overlapping voices and nowhere to rest. This should be illustrated accessibly, without a painfully loud audio effect.

The neighbor recognizes the feeling and sits down nearby.

> **Neighbor:** “It isn't asking us never to make a sound. It needs a turn.”

The family gives the Hush room. The game offers several kind approaches, none of which can fail the encounter.

### Phase 2 — Shelter

The family and their allies steady the unstable relay structures around the Elder Dragon. This is a protective action sequence, not an attack on the Hush.

Dad braces a resonator; Luke catches a drifting platform; Luna stabilizes a safe slide path; Maria cares for the sleeping dragon; Lyla keeps the world-voices coordinated. Rook opens his shield-wings as shelter instead of a cage. Nera guides the responding ships to safe positions.

### Phase 3 — Compose the final arrangement

The player brings the familiar contributions in one at a time. The market taps softly. Whisperwood answers below it. Luke's spire melody arrives, then leaves a gap. Tideglass responds. The hatchery voices join without drowning out the others. The forge accents are sparse rather than constant.

The original SOS remains recognizable. What changes is the space each voice receives.

Lyla places the familiar sound from home into the final gap, and the family supplies the resolving home note. The Hush is not erased or defeated; it finally has a place in the music.

**Payoff:** The Elder Dragon and the family's dragon wake. The restored worlds retain their own voices, and the universe has room for both music and quiet.

> **Tavi:** “That's not the song I sent.”  
> **Lyla:** “Everybody added something.”

## 11. Travel missions between the planets

Travel should alternate between wonder, action, and breathing space. Not every transition needs a fight.

### Convoy Through the Broken Belt

After the Market Moon, fly alongside Nera's cargo ship through a field of drifting ring fragments. The player pilots the dragon, clears a route, and disables capture drones. Assisted flight provides the easy route; optional maneuvers add depth. A missed maneuver causes a safe regroup rather than the loss of passengers or supplies.

The market beat becomes the first energetic flight arrangement. Each later trip adds the voices already composed.

### The Unfinished Delivery

On a later flight, Nera receives a response from an outlying shelter. A short landing lets the family deliver supplies and hear a new community join the signal. This develops the rescue network without requiring another full planet or another collectible musical layer.

### The Quiet Flight

After the hatchery reunion, allow an unhurried journey beside a few newly awake creatures. Mox's family call becomes part of the score. The moment lets the reunion land and prevents the campaign from becoming nonstop action.

### Run to the Center

After the Forge Ring, fly the route opened by the full SOS. The contributions of the rescued worlds provide clear landmarks and musical transitions. The sequence demonstrates what the whole ensemble can do before Stillheart reveals why the final arrangement needs restraint.

## 12. Returning to worlds

A completed mission should change its place permanently. Markets trade again, roots open new paths, nursery islands reconnect, and spire bridges become safe.

Returning is for play, relationships, and optional musical variation—not because neglect makes a saved planet deteriorate.

A revisit might offer a friendly cart race, a new resonator route for Luke, a soft slide course for Luna, another contribution to Lyla's archive, or a duet with local musicians. These should not require extra chapters or create chores. The ending remains reachable with the guided composition defaults.

## 13. Mission order and continuity

The written order above is an emotional presentation order, not a mandatory implementation decision.

- Earth and the Last Beacon stay first. The Market Moon introduces the planetary loop and Nera.
- Whisperwood, the Climbing Spires, the Hatchery, and Tideglass can become a selectable middle group. Important onboard discoveries should be delivered by completed-world count or explicit prerequisites, not assumed visit order.
- Rook's first encounter must happen before his Forge Ring resolution. The hatchery reunion requires Mox's family call to have been established earlier. The final breath comparison requires enough recorded clues.
- The Forge Ring follows the middle group. Stillheart follows the relay broadcast.
- A reduced production version can use fewer worlds without changing the premise. These are campaign concepts, not a requirement to build seven full planets at once.

## 14. Play and story guardrails

- The family was not chosen; their importance comes from choosing to help.
- Universe-crossing remains distinct from fast flight. The Bridge handles the crossing; ordinary interplanetary travel does not erase that distinction.
- On-foot play uses one directly controlled Line Leader when that system is available. Others follow or contribute through contextual actions. The story does not require simultaneous five-character control or forced party separation.
- The dragon has one pilot and no assigned crew-role minigames.
- Tavi, Nera, Rook, Mox, and the neighbor do not require new playable-character systems to fulfill their narrative roles.
- Combat is against capture machines and obstructive systems; encounters resolve through evasion, disabling, restraint, and recovery. Precision parrying and deeper combos are optional, not gates for young players.
- No gore, death, permanent failure, drowning mechanics, or real-time narrative deadlines. Story urgency does not become punishment for taking time to explore.
- Visual and shape cues accompany musical information. Hearing pitch differences, reading quickly, or performing on beat cannot be prerequisites for progress.
- The Hush is not secretly malicious. Restoring song and respecting quiet must both remain legitimate goals.
- The growing SOS is a shared composition. Each world's contribution survives locally and changes the family's music; it is not a resource extracted and removed.

## 15. Closing image

Back home, the block party sounds familiar: carts rolling, children laughing, someone humming in the kitchen.

Now those ordinary sounds echo the music of the rescued worlds.

The dragon curls around the house, humming the completed SOS. It pauses.

Across the open bridge, another world answers.

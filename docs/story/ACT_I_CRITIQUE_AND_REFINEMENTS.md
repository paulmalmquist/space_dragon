# Act I — Critique and Proposed Refinements

**Date:** October 28, 2026  
**Status:** PROPOSED treatment notes for owner review. Nothing here is CONFIRMED canon. [Act I](ACT_I_BUILD_THE_DRAGON.md) remains the authoritative baseline until the owner accepts specific items, which should then be recorded in `docs/decisions/`.  
**Companion:** [Act II proposal](ACT_II_ANSWER_THE_CALL.md).

Every confirmed point in Act I is kept as it is. The SOS equipment was sent on purpose and reached this family by accident. The opening is in the living room, the suits come from the backyard arrival, and the recovered components assemble a dragon design that already existed. The destination is another universe, and Lyla's suit color stays OPEN. These proposals add finesse inside those rules.

---

## 1. Critique

### What already works

- **The theme is strong and unusual.** "We weren't chosen. We were in range. Then we chose to help." This turns down the chosen-one cliché and puts the heroism in a decision the family makes. Every proposal below tries to strengthen this idea.
- **The opening is grounded.** A family at a computer, then a streak past the window. It is instantly readable for a five-year-old and needs no backstory.
- **The revelation ladder is good.** The five steps, from "something arrived" to "it leads to another universe", give the act a real mystery spine.
- **The safety and learning rules are already built into the story.** Help never withholds progress, and there's no reading at speed.

### Where it is thin

| # | Issue | Why it matters | Proposed fix (§2+) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **Every mission follows the same template.** Six missions all go: visit, minigame, take part, return. | By mission three the act feels like a checklist. Kids notice repetition fast, even if adults forgive it. | Give each mission a featured suit, a signature verb, a twist and a permanent change at home (§4). |
| C2 | **The mystery is passive.** The family receives fragments but never works anything out. | Lyla's role as the information specialist is under-used, and the reveal feels delivered rather than discovered. | Make the SOS a *song* the family rebuilds note by note, and let Lyla arrange the fragments (§3.1). |
| C3 | **There is no midpoint turn.** The arc rises steadily with no setback or surprise. | Without a turn, the finale is just "the sixth part arrives." | After the sixth module the dragon *still* can't cross. A missing seventh piece has been in the neighbor's yard all along (§5). |
| C4 | **The neighbor has no motive except dislike.** | A flat antagonist gets old and limits her role after Act I, which is flagged OPEN. | Give her a legitimate grievance and a source for her tech, and end Act I with a small moment of grace that keeps her grumpy (§5). |
| C5 | **The Core has no personality.** It is the family's main companion for the whole act. | It is the obvious "voice" of the act, and right now it is a projector. | The Core starts out mute and learns words *from the family*, which ties it to the learning rules (§3.2). |
| C6 | **The suit reveal is mechanical.** "Rescue equipment adapts" is correct but flat. | This is the act's emotional high point for kids. | The suits are made of **living material** (owner idea, §3.4) that forms around something each person was *already doing* to help (§3.3). |
| C7 | **The three-year-olds get no hero beats.** Luna and Lyla are three, and most of the big actions belong to Paul and Luke. | Two of the likely players need moments made for them. | Luna wakes the Core, Lyla plays the final message, and the preschool mission is theirs (§3–4). |
| C8 | **The revelations are tied to specific modules.** | If missions can be played in any order, the story breaks. | Tie revelations to the *number* of modules installed, not to which one (§4.1). |
| C9 | **There is no answer about home.** What happens to the house, school, and coming back? | Parents playing along will ask, and kids may worry about it. | The SOS promises a way home, the neighborhood promises to look after things, and the choice includes "Not yet" (§6). |
| C10 | **Light speed and universe-crossing are only kept apart by a rule.** | The continuity rule is right, but nothing *in the story* makes the difference visible. | Propulsion gives the dragon light speed, and the seventh "Bridge" piece is what crosses universes. The two become different objects (§5). |
| C11 | **The setting doesn't feel urgent.** There is no reason to hurry, and a real timer would be wrong for kids. | Without any pressure the stakes feel low. | A *story-only* clock: a new "star" that only the Core can see, which slowly fades. There is never a gameplay timer (§3.5). |

## 3. Throughline devices (proposed)

### 3.1 The SOS is a song

The damaged transmission's clearest part is a **short melody**, seven notes (one per recovered piece, as detailed in [music, empathy and pacing](MUSIC_EMPATHY_AND_PACING.md)), that loops and breaks off. Each module that comes home restores one more note, plus a fragment of image and voice. Kids too young to follow dialogue can still *hear* the story progress, and humming it becomes a family in-joke.

- The melody is an **unfinished musical phrase**. It ends on a note that sounds like a question. Its missing last note is the **Heartnote**, the note every song comes home to, and it's missing because Morrow holds it ([villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md)). The family supplies their own at the climax of Act III.
- Lyla's archive shows the notes as colored *and shaped* tiles (never color alone). An optional, assisted activity lets a child put recovered fragments in order. This is pattern and sequence practice, and it is never required for progress. If skipped, it is auto-arranged.
- The recurring **symbol** from the opening is a simple, traceable shape (open design). It is the icon on the town map, the mark on every pod and the dragon's chest emblem. It also gives kids an easy find-the-shape task in every mission. Proposed meaning: it's the Vieran glyph for the Heartnote, a small circle with a line curving back into it ([language and translation](LANGUAGE_AND_TRANSLATION.md)).

### 3.2 The Core learns to talk

The Core arrives damaged and can't speak our language. At first it communicates with chimes, light and gestures, like a pet. As modules return and Lyla's archive grows, it picks up words **from the family**. It starts by repeating the kids' names and simple words, and by the finale it speaks in short sentences.

- This gives the act a companion with a visible arc, and it explains why the Core can't simply tell them everything at the start.
- It also sets up the whole translation system: the English the kids teach the Core is what lets it interpret Vieran for them later ([language and translation](LANGUAGE_AND_TRANSLATION.md)).
- **Learning tie-in (optional):** a child can "teach" the Core a letter, a word or a shape. Kids often engage more when they are the teacher. Per the [learning rules](../learning/LEARNING_RULES.md), this is supported and assisted, and it is never a gate. Teaching the Core counts as practice, not as mastery.
- The Core's final name stays OPEN. One option is to let the family name it in-game once it can speak, with a default if they skip. That would be a stored setting, not canon.

### 3.3 The suits answer what you were already doing

The suits adapt to their wearers. Show that by having each suit form *around a helpful action already in progress*:

| Who | What they're doing when it happens | The suit answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paul | Lifting a fallen fence panel off the pod | Armor builds up from the gauntlets as the weight gets lighter |
| Maria | Holding a startled Luna and saying "everybody stay together" | A purple glow wraps them both, then settles onto Maria |
| Luke | Climbing the playset to see where the streaks went | Mechanical limbs unfold just as he reaches too far |
| Luna | Stomping in the sprinkler puddle near the pod | The water turns to pink goo, and she slides right up to the Core |
| Lyla | Asking "why?" over and over and pointing at every symbol | A visor drops down and her questions start being *recorded* |

Full scene beats and starting abilities: [Suit reveals and starting abilities](SUIT_REVEALS_AND_STARTING_ABILITIES.md).

This answers "why these powers?" without destiny. The suits read what each person does, and they don't know who they are. Each reveal should be **under ten seconds** and skippable after it's been seen once. Why there are exactly five suits is still OPEN. One option is that the Core carries one suit per dragon seat, and the dragon has five seats.

### 3.4 The material is alive

**Status:** owner idea, given in conversation on October 28, 2026 ("the material that makes the suits and dragon is alive"). It is **pending confirmation as canon**. All the elaborations below are PROPOSED.

The suits, the Core, the modules and the dragon are made from one **living material** (provisional name *living starmetal*; final name OPEN). It isn't metal that someone programmed. It's a material that grows, rests, heals, gets tired and reacts. It is closer to coral or a tree than to a robot.

**What this explains, without destiny:**

| Act I question | How living material answers it |
| --- | --- |
| How do the suits adapt to unknown people? | The material *grows to fit* whoever it touches, and it shapes itself around what they're doing (§3.3). It would have done the same for anyone in range. |
| How is the dragon "constructed according to a pre-existing design"? | The design lives *inside the material*, like a seed already holding the shape of its tree. The family reunites the pieces, and the material grows into the shape it always had. They didn't invent it, and they couldn't redesign it if they tried. |
| Why is the Core weak, mute and hard to understand at first? | It's hurt and half-asleep after the crash, and alone without the rest of itself. It recovers as pieces come home (§3.2). |
| Why do modules hide in odd places (the block bin, the gym roof)? | Dormant pieces curl up somewhere sheltered, like a scared animal. They didn't *choose* those places, which keeps the arrival accidental. Their faint glow and hum are the clues. |
| How does Maria "restore suit energy"? | Literally: she heals the suits. Her healing works on people, plants and the living material. |
| Where does the dragon's personality come from? | It's alive. It wakes up curious, a little clumsy and very attached to the people who brought it together. Its exact personality stays OPEN. |
| Where does the neighbor's tech come from? (§5) | The Bridge piece's material crept into her machines and grew there. That's why her robot iguanas are a little *too* lively, and why one can later change sides (Act II). |

**How it should feel:** warm to the touch, a faint slow pulse of light, and it hums the SOS melody when content. It's hurt but recoverable when damaged. Every install becomes a *reunion*: the module reaches toward the Core, the two knit together, and the hologram's section turns solid. Assembly is singing and growing, not bolting.

**Song connection:** the material responds to the melody (§3.1). Each restored note lets it grow more of the stored design. This ties the install count, the song and the dragon's visible growth into one mechanism.

**Guardrails (proposed):**
- **Never body horror or a parasite.** The material never controls, overrides or hurts the wearer. Suits retract on request, and helmets open on request. A child's "off" always works.
- **It offers and the family accepts.** It reaches out, but nothing joins without someone choosing to touch it. This fits Act I's rule against secret abduction.
- **It can be hurt, but nothing dies in Act I.** Damage shows as dimming and slowness that healing reverses. No graphic damage.
- **Separate state from animation:** a suit's "mood" glow and breathing pulse are presentation only. They are not health or progress state, per the [architecture](../engineering/ARCHITECTURE.md).

**Gameplay notes:**
- Paul's "repair" becomes *helping it knit back together*. The contextual action is the same, but the animation and sound read as healing rather than welding.
- An optional care moment after each mission: comforting the dragon, patting a suit, humming with the Core. It's creative play with no failure state, and **never a recurring chore** (no feeding meters, no decay while away).
- Lyla's scans show the material's mood (sleepy, happy, scared), which gives her another readable, non-text information role.

### 3.5 The fading star (story-only urgency)

When the Navigation module comes home, the planning station shows a **new point of light** in the night sky. Only Lyla's visor and the Core can see it. This is the opening to the other universe. Between missions it dims slightly, and the Core chirps worriedly at it. It's dimming because it's the last gap in Morrow's note, and he is filling it ([villain arc](VILLAIN_ARC.md)).

- It gives the act a gentle reason to keep going.
- **There is never a gameplay timer.** The star cannot close during Act I, however long the player takes. It's mood, not a fail state.

## 4. Mission refinements

### 4.1 Ordering rule

- **Mission 1 is fixed** and doubles as the tutorial: the girls' preschool, Assembly Module.
- **Missions 2–5 can be played in any order.**
- **Mission 6 (Propulsion) unlocks after any four**, because it's the big set piece.
- SOS fragments, notes and Core vocabulary are delivered by **install count** (first install, second install…), not by module. Each module still has its own *home demo* (wings flex, cockpit lights, and so on). This keeps the revelation ladder coherent whatever order the family chooses.

### 4.2 Per-mission treatment

Each mission has one **featured suit** (everyone still comes along), one signature **verb** and a **twist** that breaks the fetch pattern. It also leaves a **permanent change at home** the player can see.

| # | Place (fictionalized) | Module | Featured | Verb | Twist | Home change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Girls' preschool | Assembly | Luna & Lyla | **Sort** | The module shattered into glowing pieces that are now mixed in with the classroom's building blocks. Find which blocks are "space blocks" (shape matching), then chase the iguana that grabbed the last one. | The Core can now make **Builder Bugs**, tiny helper bots that do every later docking animation and can be sent to help neighbors. |
| 2 | Luke's school | Navigation | Luke | **Climb** | The module landed on the gym roof and is projecting a strange star map onto the ceiling. Kids inside think it's a planetarium show. Luke has to reach it before recess ends (a story beat, not a timer). | The planning station becomes a **star map**, and the fading star appears (§3.5). |
| 3 | Big warehouse store | Power | Paul | **Lift / haul** | The pod is wedged among the giant pallets, and it's *heavy*. Paul lifts, then the family races it home on a flatbed cart while neighbors cheer from driveways. | The Core powers up fully. It now **glows at night**, and the street notices. |
| 4 | Neighborhood grocery | Life Support | Maria | **Care** | The module was **carrying a passenger**: a small sleeping creature from the other universe in a stasis bubble, scared and weak. Maria's healing wakes it. (See §4.3.) | The creature now lives at the house, and it hums the SOS melody. |
| 5 | Big-box store | Wing Control | Luke + Luna | **Chase / slip** | The neighbor's robot iguanas got there first. Mosquito drones run interference in the parking lot, and Luna's goo makes the getaway bots spin out. Lyla scans an iguana and sees **the Core's symbol glowing inside it**. | The wings on the half-built dragon flex for the first time, and the neighbor sees it. |
| 6 | Fictional rocket facility | Propulsion | Paul, then everyone | **Test / launch** | Paul's coworkers (fictional) help run a safe engine test. The armored alligator ambushes, and the first **full five-suit teamwork** encounter uses the v0.1 example: Lyla finds the core, Luna makes it overshoot, Luke reaches the core, Maria shields and Paul holds the exit. | The dragon's **body stands complete** in the cul-de-sac. Everyone expects it to wake. It doesn't. → §5 |

### 4.3 The passenger (new proposal)

The Life Support module was built to carry living things, and it arrives carrying one. A small creature (design OPEN; something kid-sized and cuddly with a glow) has been asleep in a stasis bubble since it was sent.

- **Why it matters:** it makes "someone needs help" *tangible*. Kids will care about it far more than about a transmission.
- **Gameplay:** it follows the family around at home (as an animation, not a follower system), reacts to the song and nuzzles the Core. Optional care moments (feeding, comforting) are creative play with no right answer.
- **Story function:** it's the first living proof of the other universe, and in Act II it is the family's guide and introduction. It must **not** imply that the family was chosen. It was just in the module.
- Its name is OPEN. The kids naming it is a natural option.

### 4.4 Neighborhood allies

The supportive neighbors (all fictional) each help on one return trip, so the cul-de-sac becomes a cast:

- A neighbor with a truck or trailer hauls the Power pod the last stretch.
- A neighbor who bakes brings celebration snacks to every install. This is a running gag, and the snacks get more elaborate.
- The street's kids cheer from bikes and later get Builder Bugs to play with.
- A retired neighbor lends tools and tells unbelievable "back in my day" stories.

Every return home ends with a **short install party**: docking, the new note, the home change and one neighbor beat. The rhythm makes coming home feel like the reward.

## 5. Movement 3 — "Across the Street" (new scene A1-06b, proposed)

The new midpoint turn and the source of the neighbor's tech.

**Setup.** All six modules are installed. The dragon's full body stands in the cul-de-sac, and the whole street gathers. The Core powers it up, and the eyes flicker, then go dark. The melody plays and stops one note short of where it should end. The Core, now able to say a little, says something like: *"Not… all. One more. Here."* It points across the street.

**The reveal.** One more piece, the **Bridge** (provisional name), fell the night it all began. It landed in the neighbor's backyard and wrecked something she cares about, perhaps her prize garden. Nobody asked whether she was all right. She has been using the glowing piece to power her robot iguanas and drones. **This is proposed as the source of her tech, which Act I leaves OPEN.**

- Her complaints have a real grievance underneath. She is still overreacting, which is the joke, but she isn't just mean.
- **This piece crosses universes. Propulsion only gives the dragon light speed.** That makes the Act I continuity rule *visible*: the dragon can fly and go fast, but it can't cross until the Bridge is installed.

**The encounter.** The family goes over to ask. She refuses and activates her biggest machine yet: the armored alligator, upgraded and powered by the Bridge. It's a playful boss in her backyard. It slips, gets gooed and then disabled, and nothing is broken that matters.

**The resolution is empathy, not victory.** With the machine stopped, the Bridge is exposed, but the family doesn't just grab it.

- Maria heals the damaged garden (her healing works on plants too).
- Luna, or whichever child is playing, offers an apology for the mess.
- Lyla replays the archive recording of the night the piece fell, and the neighbor sees it wasn't the family's fault either.

She hands it over. Then, in character: *"This doesn't mean I approve of the dragon."*

**Why this is better:** the act's climax becomes a decision about *how* to win, which echoes the act's theme of choosing to help. It also sets up the neighbor's Act II role (see the Act II proposal).

## 6. Movement 4 — finale refinements (A1-07, A1-08)

### A1-07 Assembly complete

- The Bridge goes in last. The Builder Bugs swarm, the wings unfold over the cul-de-sac and **five seats light up**. The dragon's eyes open properly this time.
- The dragon's first act is to look at each family member in turn, then up at the fading star. Its personality stays OPEN. A single expressive sound is enough here.
- The whole street is out. The baking neighbor brings a cake shaped like the dragon. The neighbor watches from her porch.

### A1-08 The full message

- Install count is complete, so the SOS plays **in full** for the first time. Image, voice and the whole melody come through, translated by the Core in its now-short sentences. The melody still ends on its question.
- **Lyla plays it.** It's her archive, and the full message is a three-year-old's hero beat.
- The message includes, in some form: *we need help; anyone who can hear this; this is how to come; this is how to get home.* **The promise of a way home is new and proposed.** It answers C9 and keeps the departure from feeling like abduction.
- The passenger creature hears the full song and sings back, and it plainly wants to go home.

### The decision

- An actual family moment, not a cutscene button. Each family member puts a hand on the Core. The player may choose **"Answer the call"** or **"Not yet."**
  - **Not yet** keeps the player at home in a free-play cul-de-sac (fly short hops, replay missions, visit neighbors) until they're ready. The fading star never closes.
  - **Answer** leads to the goodbye beat.
- **Goodbye beat:** neighbors promise to water plants and watch the house. Then the neighbor crosses the street and says something like *"Somebody has to keep an eye on things."* It plays like reluctant care, and it is also a setup (see Act II).
- **Closing image:** the dragon rises off the cul-de-sac, the house lights get small, and the fading star opens into a ring of light. **Cut.**

## 7. Open decisions raised by these proposals

- **Confirm as canon:** the material that makes the suits and dragon is alive (owner idea, §3.4). Once confirmed, record it in `docs/decisions/`. Also decide the material's name and look, and whether it has its own will or is purely responsive.

- Accept or reject: the SOS melody, the talking Core, the passenger creature, the Bridge as a seventh piece, and the neighbor's grievance as her tech source.
- The symbol's design; the melody (composition, number of notes); the creature's design and name; the Core's name.
- Whether the "Not yet" free-play mode exists, or whether the decision scene simply waits.
- The final mission count. This document proposes 6 + 1, which can shrink for production. The first playable should still be one mission (see [the current milestone](../plans/CURRENT_MILESTONE.md)).

## 8. Production notes

- This changes nothing in the SD-M1 First Contact scope. The proposals that touch the opening are suit-forming gestures (§3.3), the symbol and the first notes of the melody. All of them can use placeholders.
- Install-count delivery (§4.1) is a **state design decision** worth making early. It keeps story progress in game state rather than in specific cinematics, consistent with the [interaction contracts](../gameplay/INTERACTION_CONTRACTS.md).
- The Bridge reveal must not "un-install" anything. The six modules stay installed. The dragon is just not finished yet.

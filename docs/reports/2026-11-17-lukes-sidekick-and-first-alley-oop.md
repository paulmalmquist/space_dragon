## Review and release

- Implemented outcome: Luke's Arena has a sidekick, Dad as a placeholder figure, who follows on his own, stuns a robot when asked and throws Luke for one alley-oop; the same rules in engine-free C# with recorded-run parity; a Unity figure and button that compile outside the Editor.
- Coverage not performed: everything inside Unity; any real device; human play; human art or sound review; agent-behavior tests.
- Human review: NOT REVIEWED.
- Publication / deployment approval: NOT GRANTED. Nothing was deployed. No likeness, voice, purchase or new external service.
- Known gaps and risks:
  - **Not asked for by name.** The owner asked "What's next"; an agent chose this. Who the sidekick is, what his button means and whether he should help unasked are the owner's to decide.
  - **Strong and untuned.** The dive powers the nearest robot down outright every six seconds. With the action button as well, the ring will be easier than before, and it was already easy.
  - Standing still to be thrown, while robots are winding up, may not suit a child in the middle of a fight. Moving, the button stuns instead; which of the two a press will do is shown only by the chip and the arrow.
  - He does nothing to robots unless asked, so "mostly autonomous" means following, making room and getting ready. Robots ignore him entirely.
  - The figure is primitive shapes and five procedural poses. He steps back when walked into but can still be overlapped in a corner.
  - Three layers now sit on work that has never been opened in Unity or played by a person. The [milestone](../plans/CURRENT_MILESTONE.md) asks that nothing more be added to the arena until someone has played it.
  - The combat notebook's 43 specified cases remain NOT TESTED; "each alley-oop resolves once, including under a double-tap" is covered here in spirit by the mash test, not executed as that case is written.
- Recovery / rollback: additive. Deleting `sidekick: SIDEKICK` from `ARENA_LAYOUT` in `src/game/arena/arena.ts` and running `npm run unity:arena:export` takes him out of play in both runtimes: his chip hides and his button does nothing. His tests and the five new recorded runs would then have to be removed as well. Reverting the commits that carry this receipt does all of that at once. Arena saves are untouched either way; nothing about him is stored.
